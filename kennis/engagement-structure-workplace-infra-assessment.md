# Engagement structure: workplace and infrastructure assessment

My standard shape for a workplace/infrastructure advisory engagement, reusable across clients. The deliverable is an advisory report, explicitly not a quote ("globaal voorstel, geen offerte"). All figures in it are indicative, no rights can be derived from them.

## Phases
1. **Goal definition.** State the objective in one line: deliver advice on a renewed workplace concept that fits the client's functional need, IT strategy and IT policy. Scope is agreed up front; the assessment is tuned to that scope.
2. **Intake by interview.** Gather information through a set of interviews plus any other available material (current workplace concept, application landscape, specific requirements and wishes). See my separate note on the interview/assessment method.
3. **IST analysis (current state).** Document the environment as it is. See the IST/SOLL report-skeleton note.
4. **SOLL advice (target state) as two scenarios.** Always work out two scenarios, not one. Each scenario must satisfy (largely) the requirements and address the listed current challenges. Where applicable, attach an indicative investment estimate. See the two-scenario note.
5. **0.9 presentation.** Present the report in outline to a client-selected audience. The key technology choices are explained functionally, with full room for questions and feedback. Technical depth can sit in the report, but the report must state that everything will be explained functionally at the 0.9 presentation.
6. **1.0 finalization.** Rework the 0.9 into a 1.0 based on the feedback, then re-share with all stakeholders. Only after a scenario is chosen do I produce the detailed technical design plus a migration plan.

## Document discipline (house style for the report)
- Always have at least one colleague review before it goes out.
- Think in functionality, not just technology. Explain loaded terms (innovation, flexibility) or they say nothing.
- Network drawings, line diagrams and third-party explanations go in as appendices, not inline, or they become unreadable.
- Keep naming, abbreviations and number formatting consistent throughout (numbers as words OR digits, never both).
- Cite the source on every image (copyright).
- Round all amounts, including calculator output, to whole numbers for readability.
- New chapters start on a new page; use page breaks liberally so the document survives later edits.
- End the document with a side-by-side comparison of the two scenarios.
- Always send as PDF. If Word is required, embed the non-standard font or the layout breaks at the recipient.

## Distribution
Internal review on a need-to-know basis; externally only to the commissioning client. Further distribution only with the client's approval. Mark concept versions as confidential and for evaluation/approval only; note that the assessment is not legal advice.
