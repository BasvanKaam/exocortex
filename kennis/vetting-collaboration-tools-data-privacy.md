# Vetting Collaboration Tools for Data and Privacy

When a rush to collaboration/video software happens, the worrying gap is how organizations handle the security and privacy aspects of these solutions (often: not at all). Capacity figures (e.g. 'Zoom supports up to 49 people in a video meeting') say nothing about what the tool does with your data in the background.

What to do, even mid-crisis:
- Most organizations already have a policy on how (sensitive) data should be handled, even if a formal home-working policy does not yet exist; lean on it.
- Find out how each provider handles data: contact the vendor, an independent IT partner, read their privacy policy. Do not rely blindly on internet articles, the neighbor's tips (however well-meant), or a direct competitor.
- Ask the core questions: how is the data secured, where is it stored, what may the software vendor do with it, who owns the data?
- For new tooling like Teams, decide governance up front: who supervises correct use, who may create pages/groups (permanent or temporary), who can access or see what, is a back-up considered, what does the rights/permission structure look like?
