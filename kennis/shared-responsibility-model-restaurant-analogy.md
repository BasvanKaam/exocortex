# Shared Responsibility Model (Restaurant Analogy)

Bas demystifies the shared responsibility model: it seems complicated but is really about who does what, what you arrange yourself versus what the Cloud provider does. The layers run from your data and applications down to physical systems and network. Moving left toward SaaS, you arrange less; moving right toward On-Prem, your own responsibility grows.

- SaaS (e.g. Microsoft 365, Google Workspace): you are only responsible for what you put in (your data) and who has access; the provider runs the app and secures the underlying infrastructure.
- PaaS: you build your own apps on the provider's platform; you handle code/application, data and account management; the provider keeps servers, storage and network running.
- IaaS: more control; you choose OS, applications and network setup; the provider handles physical hardware (servers, network).
- On-Premises: you arrange everything yourself, from physical datacenters (power, maintenance, cooling), servers and network to applications. Maximum control, but all burdens on your shoulders.

Bas's analogy: it's like eating. Either you go to the supermarket, assemble it yourself, cook it, clean up (On-Prem), or you eat à-la-carte and pick from the menu while everything, down to the dishwashing, is arranged for you (SaaS). The key is knowing exactly where your responsibilities end and your Cloud partner's begin, to avoid misunderstandings. Model source: Microsoft Learn.
