# Systeembeheerder 2.0: from technician to functional broker

Bas's proposition on how the system administrator role is changing.

The forces hollowing out classic IT-as-a-service:
- Front end: BYOD (Bring Your Own Device). Increasingly assertive, digitally-savvy users want to choose their own tools; organisations let people buy, use and manage their preferred devices, then consume IT as a service on top (apps and data made available regardless of device).
- Back end: ever more traditional services delivered as cloud services — software, backup, even whole virtual infrastructures — often more efficiently and cost-effectively, as these services have matured.
- Outsourcing: server and application management shifts to cloud providers; IT has become so complex and business-critical that internal admins can no longer master every detail, so specialist external providers are called in.

The consequence: the technical, almost artisanal craft of the old sysadmin will inevitably fade. Knowing every checkbox in the backup tool matters less; functional, effective deployment of IT matters more (e.g. colleagues' functional wishes in the CRM package, the details of the SLA with the cloud provider).

Sysadmin 2.0: technical-expertise demand inside the organisation drops, replaced by new responsibilities centred on the functional side and on guarding maintenance/support contracts. The role becomes less technically challenging but holds an enormous opportunity — the new sysadmin becomes the key to deploying IT effectively for the business, the crucial link translating business needs and wishes to the IT partner(s).
