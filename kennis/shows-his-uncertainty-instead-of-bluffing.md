# Shows his 90% certainty and his confusion rather than bluffing

The Scout 3.0 post (May 2017) contains one of the clearest examples of Bas's intellectual-honesty stance in technical writing. On the question of exactly how Scout vs the CTX200341 PowerShell script each drive Logman, he refuses to pretend certainty: 'I've done some research and I've also spoken to a couple of Citrix technicians I'm still not a 100% sure on what I'm about to share next, though I'm probably close to 90 or 95% or so.' He tags individual sentences with '(assumption)', dedicates a whole section titled 'Confused' to walking through the wrong mental model he initially had (thinking the script used CDF-Control under the hood) and how he corrected it, and thanks the Citrix engineers who set him straight. His position: it is better to show the seams of your understanding, label assumptions explicitly, and narrate the correction, than to present a confident but possibly wrong answer. This 'never bluff, flag the uncertainty' instinct is a durable signature of how he works.
