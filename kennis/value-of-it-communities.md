# Bas on the value of IT communities

Chapter 13 is Bas's personal case for communities, which he says hold a wealth of information. Over his career he has been involved in dozens - as participant, organiser and founder - and learned a lot from all of it. The chapter focuses on external communities (events you attend, webinars, podcasts) but he notes the concept works inside company walls too.

Why communities matter, especially in IT: you learn from others' experience (someone may have used a technology you're discovering, or solved a problem you're hitting), saving time and giving fresh perspectives; they keep you up to date as technology changes razor-fast, via meetups, webinars and subject-matter-expert blogs; you meet people with the same passion and drive, leading to valuable connections, friendships and collaboration, plus visibility in your field (personal branding); and you get fast answers and feedback from people with diverse backgrounds.

He stresses a community is not one-way traffic - by sharing knowledge you help others and learn yourself (he references the learning pyramid from the start of his book). It supports you so you don't have to do everything alone, keeps you developing, and helps you find enjoyment in your work, regardless of experience level. He admits self-critically that he has spent too little time on this in recent years.
