# Paper conclusion: Cloud only? Maybe never

Bas's paper concludes that the cloud promise — cheaper, safer, infinitely scalable, simple, always and everywhere available — proves only partly true, and that's hard to deny.

Key conclusion points:
- The cloud too suffers outages and downtime, with big impact; organisations hand over part of their control and are then at the mercy of the knowledge, skill and SLAs of Microsoft, AWS, GCP or another third party.
- In parts of the Netherlands and the US, cloud datacenters already hit physical limits and power consumption explodes — so badly that further expansion/new construction was (temporarily) curbed; part of the Haarlemmermeer datacenters even runs on emergency power. If there's a crisis, datacenters are shut down and the public gets priority "as it should be."
- The 94%+ of companies worldwide using cloud are mostly using services like email, Office 365, SAP or HR packages — that's something quite different from moving the bulk of a datacenter to the cloud.
- The huge amount of legacy/traditional software plus deep-rooted dependencies will have major influence for years. Many experience on-prem/hybrid as safer — perhaps a false sense of security, but it counts.
- This resonates with the big providers too: more cloud-driven services come on-premises (AWS Outposts, Azure Stack Hub). Containers + Kubernetes enable moving machines, data and apps between clouds and between cloud and on-prem; Azure Arc aims at global governance/management from a single console.
- Resistance among traditional IT staff and the knowledge gap will likely disappear within 3-5 years — the power of repetition does its work. Slowing, not blocking.

Final line: "Cloud only? No, that won't happen soon, maybe never!" Bas notes he deliberately added no key takeaways or important questions to this chapter, and that the references are formatted differently because that was a requirement of the original assignment.
