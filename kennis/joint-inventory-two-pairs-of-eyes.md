# Joint Inventory: Two Pairs of Eyes Beat One

Bas's preferred intake technique for the SCAF Insight phase: run the application, system and data inventory **together with the client**, not as an outside-in desk exercise. From the proposals: "Twee paar ogen zien meer dan een. Terwijl de een dicteert noteert de ander." One person dictates from system knowledge, the other documents, and gaps surface in real time.

Why he insists on it:
- The internal admin who knows the current environment is "goud waard" during a cloud transition. As an outsider you cannot say anything sensible about dependencies, volumes or exceptions without that tacit knowledge, so trying to inventory from the outside is near-pointless.
- Existing client documentation (application and system overviews) is almost always somewhat dated and not 100% complete. The joint pass corrects it.
- Working side by side also produces an early feel for the possibilities, which can be documented on the spot, blurring the line into the next (Execution) phase.

Scoping he uses: with cooperation and dedicated time, the joint inventory of a medium-complexity environment fits in roughly **three to four days**; finishing earlier is a bonus. He explicitly reassures the client that the dependency checklist "lijkt veel, is het niet."

The consultant's job is to state up front exactly which information is needed to make a grounded decision and a detailed proposal; the client decides how involved they want to be. Often a single workshop's worth of information is enough for Salomon to proceed largely independently.

When detailed data per application is missing, a simple yes/no (J/N) against each dependency criterion suffices to move forward.

*Bron: geanonimiseerde aanpak uit een Salomon-IT klantopdracht (private-equity / investment firm (and, in the same engagement file, a food-research institute) — both small-to-mid organizations evaluating a managed-services and cloud migration).*
