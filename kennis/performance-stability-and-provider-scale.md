# Performance and Stability: You Trade Control for Provider Scale

Bas's nuanced take on the 'performance and stability' concern. Yes — when something goes wrong in a cloud environment you are not 'in control' and are at the mercy of your cloud service provider, with only a limited toolset to fix the issue (if it can even be fixed). That's a fair point.

However, very few companies worldwide invest in datacenter security at that scale (he cites Azure at 2-4 billion per year), spreading systems across multiple physical racks, buildings, and geographically separated zones for high availability. No half measures are taken — unhappy customers hamper growth. For most organisations such a setup is simply not feasible. The flip side: when something does break on a cloud platform, hundreds or thousands of companies can be affected at once. Still, Bas observes that the interval between complications that actually impact users' daily work is much lower in the cloud, precisely because of those extreme measures.

He also notes every provider has its own SLA — often one per service. If a service is unavailable, this is usually compensated as cloud credits (virtual money).
