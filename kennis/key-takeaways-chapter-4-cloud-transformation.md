# Key Takeaways Chapter 4: Cloud Transformation Beyond Technology

Bas's closing takeaways for chapter 4 argue cloud transformation goes far beyond technology — it is about culture, processes and collaboration:

- The move to cloud impacts the whole organisation (culture). Traditional structures give way to flexible ways of working like DevOps and self-steering teams, letting organisations respond faster.
- A key shift is from large one-off IT investments (Capex) to spreading costs over time (Opex): you pay for usage.
- Success requires IT and business to collaborate; the traditional gap between them must be closed. Involving employees, training, guidance and clear communication remove uncertainty — e.g. fear of losing control over infrastructure can be partly addressed by clear agreements with cloud providers.
- Cloud brings challenges like security and compliance in an increasingly remote world, raising the chance of data leaks and non-compliance. **Shadow IT** (use of unapproved cloud services) is a double phenomenon: a risk on one hand, but valuable insight into how employees really use technology on the other.
- IT's role changes drastically — from management to acting as a 'regisseur' connecting the right cloud services to business needs. New skills (DevOps, cloud management, security) grow in importance.
- Cloud opens new business models: from selling products to offering services, from ownership to usage. This requires smart choices to avoid risks like vendor lock-in, and a well-considered exit strategy.

His reframe: even Shadow IT can be seen as an opportunity, not just a risk.
