# Hardening Has Shifted Into Pre-Sales, and Training Must Account for It

Bas's stance: hardening NME was traditionally a post-sales activity, but customer demand has increasingly pulled it into the pre-sales / PoV phase. That has a real commercial cost: it consumes effort during the 30-day free trial and extends the overall sales cycle. He argues this impact must be made explicit in enablement, so sales engineers and customers recognize the trade-off rather than being surprised by it. Tied to this is his view that NME being un-hardened by default is a defensible design choice (it gets a working install first), but one that demands a standardized, documented hardening path.
