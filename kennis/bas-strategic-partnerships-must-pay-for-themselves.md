# Bas: strategic tech partnerships must make the work easier and pay for themselves

When Bas advises an IT service provider on which vendor and technology partnerships to take on, he applies a clear filter: a partnership only earns its place if it **actually makes the work easier and pays for itself**. He is explicit that the point is not the logo or the relationship for its own sake — it is a small number of *strategic* partnerships chosen because they demonstrably reduce effort and return their cost. A management/automation layer that lets a small team deliver a cloud platform without deep specialist headcount is his go-to example of a partnership that meets the bar.

This is a position, not a neutral fact: Bas pushes back on partnering for breadth or for marketing optics. The default question he forces is 'does this make our delivery genuinely easier, and does it earn its keep?' — and if the answer isn't a clear yes, it doesn't belong in the strategy.

It connects to how he frames a new offering commercially: the proposition is a cost-and-ROI story, so the tooling and partnerships underneath it have to carry their own ROI too.
