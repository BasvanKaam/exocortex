# Bas: the cloud doesn't make people redundant, it frees them for better work

The payoff of Bas's 'Angst' story. What the resistant IT team initially failed to see was that cloud actually gave them more room for more interesting work and time to study. After migration they no longer wrestled with the same issues daily: system updates were simplified, back-ups ran automatically, scaling capacity was a few clicks (or fully automated), and the phone stayed home at weekends.

Concrete results: one employee who had been frustrated for years that he could never develop his ideas finally got to draft a new security policy; another optimised company workflows, improving other departments' productivity. Bas calls this a positive snowball effect ('positief sneeuwbaleffect').

His key reframe and lesson: automation doesn't make people redundant - it creates opportunities for more strategic and creative work, letting staff act proactively instead of reactively. But this perspective must be communicated clearly and in time, right from the start, to remove resistance. The story you take people through should be: not that the cloud comes and takes your job, but that the cloud makes your work more fun, more challenging and more valuable.
