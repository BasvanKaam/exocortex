# Legacy x86 app usage in enterprises is rising, not falling

Bas argues, against the common assumption, that the use of traditional/legacy x86 applications inside enterprise environments has increased in recent years, not decreased. Because so many applications are still written for a desktop operating system, this is one of his core justifications for why desktop virtualization (and WVD specifically) stays relevant rather than being a transitional technology on its way out.

He pairs this with a pragmatic stance on Windows 7 migration: take your time phasing out Windows 7, since studies show on average around 50% of estates were still on it, for understandable reasons (no time, resources or money; apps dependent on Windows 7; user habit and uncertainty). The free extended support for Windows 7 under WVD is what buys that time.
