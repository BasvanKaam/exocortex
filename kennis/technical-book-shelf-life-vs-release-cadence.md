# Why a deep-architecture book outlives a rapid release cadence

Durable principle (framed by Bas in 2017 around the XenDesktop/XenApp 7.x release cycle of every three to four months): a book built around the underlying architecture rather than around install-and-configure steps keeps most of its value across versions. His estimate was that up to 80% of the content remained relevant from one 7.x version to the next, and that even the 'outdated' sections still taught readers about the evolution of the products. Two reinforcing factors: most companies do not upgrade the moment a new version ships, and Long Term Service Releases (then 7.6) pin a large install base to one version for years.

The transferable lesson: write to the architecture and the why, not the UI of the moment, if you want shelf life.
