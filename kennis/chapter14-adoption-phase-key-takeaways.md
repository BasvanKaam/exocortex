# Key Takeaways: the adoption phase is a beginning, not an end

Bas's chapter Key Takeaways frame the adoption phase as the last but absolutely not least step of a cloud transition — it is about optimisation, securing gains (borging) and continuous improvement, and it does *not* stop at implementation. An iterative approach (agile, PDCA: Plan-Do-Check-Act) keeps teams learning and adapting so both technical and cultural changes are embraced.

Core points he stresses:
- **Avoid unnecessary cost**: automate tasks and remove superfluous resources to raise cost-efficiency; keep components and systems up to date for security and future-readiness.
- **Optimise management processes** (incident, change, problem management) so they fit the dynamic nature of the cloud; formally hand over migrated components and delivered projects to operational teams, creating clear ownership for management, monitoring and lifecycle management.
- **Ambassadors persist** in this phase, driving acceptance and supporting communication and knowledge transfer; a solid skill-readiness plan ensures staff have the right competencies.
- **Reporting and transparency** build trust, engagement and support; as adoption proceeds new use-cases emerge across HR, finance and sales.
- **Strong governance** safeguards compliance and security; detailed documentation of processes, configurations and decisions underpins knowledge transfer and continuity.
- **KPIs** measure cloud performance and surface improvement points.

The headline reframe: adoption is not the end of a cloud journey but the start of continuous learning and adaptation.
