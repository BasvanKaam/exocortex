# Operating a Community Advocate Program

Reusable operating principles for running a vendor community-advocate program (drawn from Bas's NVP playbook). Captures the method, not the internal roster.

## Recognition badge / logo
- Put the year of recognition on the badge (not a founding year).
- Add a small star per year awarded, as the VMware programs do. This gives renewed members something fresh to share each year, a reason to update their logo wherever they display it, and visible proof of tenure.

## Membership cadence
- Add new members at will; no fixed intake dates.
- For the first ~25 members, ask each to write a short self-intro and introduce them via a blog post. This visibly demonstrates the program's growth and popularity.
- Track renewal dates and notify members about a week before expiry. Splitting the year into two renewal anchors (e.g. Dec 1 and Jul 1) keeps it manageable.

## Metrics
- Decide up front what single metric you monitor, for both members and potential members.
- Consistency of contribution is a more important metric than raw numbers. Use it (rather than one-off volume) to decide things like who gets the conference invite, and consider making selection a contest to reward sustained engagement.

## Perks to prepare
- Conference invite (regional: US or Europe), with clear, fair selection criteria set at the start of the year.
- A welcome kit.
- Stickers (gated on the finalized logo).

The through-line: make recognition renewable, visible, and earned through consistency.
