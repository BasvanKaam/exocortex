# XpressLabs facilitation craft: how Bas runs the room

The first-person version of the in-a-box format adds the **delivery mechanics** Bas uses to run a hands-on session smoothly. These are the practical facilitation moves layered on top of the lab sequence (the method itself lives in the in-a-box-method note).

## Running-the-room techniques
- **Mirror slides into Teams.** A Teams invite goes out beforehand and slides are followed along in Teams, so attendees sitting far away can read small fonts, and so links (lab URLs) can be pasted into chat instead of dictated. Removes friction before the labs even start.
- **Keep a live progress slide.** A high-level slide listing all the labs stays on screen the whole time; he strikes through each lab as it's finished. The room (and he) can see exactly where they are. Visible progress as a motivator.
- **Demonstrate first, always.** Each lab is demonstrated first and/or attendees click along while he explains.
- **Tailor to the partner.** Agenda is flexed by partner, their situation, and whatever was agreed beforehand; he can deep-dive specific topics on request.
- **Mid-session reflection, not just at the end.** He applies a brief review-and-reflect after certain labs as well as at the close, on purpose, because repetition is good.
- **Give an exam ultimatum.** Because the NME-200 exam takes real time, he tells people up front there may not be room to sit it during the day, and sets an expectation for completing it after.

## Why it works
The through-line is reducing every small friction (can't read the slide, can't type the URL, don't know where we are, didn't retain the concept) so attendees stay in the hands-on flow. Reusable as a checklist for delivering any live technical workshop.

Note: this format had been delivered to multiple partners across the Netherlands and Belgium; the pipeline/leads and QBR specifics in the source are internal and were not recorded.
