# Lack of overview, knowledge, governance and Shadow IT

Bas's paper frames the governance gap with the saying "unable to see the forest for the trees." Organisations simply don't know where to start, which creates dangers. Seeking advice, training staff, starting small and experimenting all help — it slows things but isn't insurmountable. The real danger lies in overall governance and in the very ease the cloud brings.

Supporting data (Nutanix Enterprise Cloud Index 2019): 32% say they lack the knowledge in-house; 53% call this a top concern for (hybrid) cloud adoption in coming years; 91% say their organisation invests in training staff, while 49% admit they don't today possess the skills to design, implement and maintain a cloud environment.

**Shadow IT** — cloud's ease lets ordinary users procure services (free or paid) for business use without realising the impact. Eventually someone must take responsibility, preferably the internal IT department — nobody knows the business, processes, admin, risks and dangers better. This won't stop cloud use, but until the governance model is in order companies are wise to postpone or limit cloud use, to avoid disappointment and to keep trust from taking a dent.

Bas's key reframe: ultimately cloud is just a way of doing IT — a tool in the toolbox, a wheelbarrow and nothing more. Cloud is not a strategy in itself but part of the broader IT strategy.
