# The 'Cloud because of the Cloud' syndrome and poor migration planning

Bas identifies a common failure: migrating to the Cloud without a clear strategy or thorough analysis of business needs, including the current application and data landscape. Customers often pre-decide what they want to do and how, then later realise their plans do not match the reality of their operations or IT architecture - leading to inefficiencies, higher costs and even security risks. Many companies pick the Cloud just because it is the latest trend or because competitors are migrating. He names his own answer to this: the Step-Cloud Adoptie Framework helps here.

He calls this the 'Cloud vanwege de Cloud'-syndroom (Cloud because of the Cloud syndrome), which leads to rushed decisions without sufficient thought about the real pros and cons. Organisations should choose Cloud technology because it fits their business goals and operational requirements, not because others are doing it.

A related cause is the lack of a clear definition of what 'the Cloud' is (ranging from IaaS to SaaS and everything between). This ambiguity means companies do not fully understand what they are buying, and complicates decisions because different departments hold different expectations and definitions. His advice: base Cloud choices on clear, well-defined business needs rather than being swept along by buzzwords or market trends. And when in doubt, bring in a trusted partner with proven expertise and track record - it costs extra time and possibly money, but that does not outweigh the potential problems later if you skip it.
