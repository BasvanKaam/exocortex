# Bas: 'Wegwerp IT' and the Lift-and-Shift Caution

Bas coins serverless/ephemeral functions as **"Wegwerp IT"** ("throwaway IT") - code that spins up, does its job and disappears.

His caution on **lift and shift** (just picking up your current environment and moving it to the Cloud unchanged): it probably won't solve your problems and may actually create more challenges. He invokes "garbage in, garbage out." The upside he concedes: it gets you quickly familiar with the Cloud platform, so you learn a lot and may make progress fast; fun to test, perhaps.

He stresses Cloud-native technologies apply mainly to newly developed software/infrastructure or when existing software is rewritten, which takes time and specific expertise. You must carefully weigh the added value of a full rebuild; sometimes rewriting makes sense, not always. He points to the **6 R's** (Rehost, Replatform, Repurchasing, Refactor, Retire, Retain) as the decision framework, covered in a later chapter.

He closes with a quote from Chris Hoff: "If you think you suck now, you'll be pleasantly surprised when you move to the Cloud."
