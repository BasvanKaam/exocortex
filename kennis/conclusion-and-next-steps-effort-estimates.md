# The 'conclusion and next steps' pattern with split effort estimates

In his advisory reports Bas does not write one big conclusion at the end. Every substantive section (technical dependencies, application requirements, directory/security, licensing, training and guidance, etc.) closes with its own short **"Conclusion and next steps"** block. Each block does three things: states the finding for that topic, names what still has to be investigated or decided, and gives an effort estimate.

The estimate is always split in two:

- **Expected effort to work out and plan** (the design/preparation hours).
- **Expected effort to implement and apply** (the execution hours).

Keeping those two separate lets the client see the thinking cost versus the doing cost per topic, and it makes the final roll-up honest. At the end he aggregates all sections into a **totals** chapter: plan-hours + implement-hours summed, multiplied by an explicitly stated blended rate (he used an average rate as a placeholder when the executing party and tariff were not yet known), plus a separate licensing line and a separate hardware line, then a grand total and an estimated lead time.

Guardrails he attaches to every number:

- He states the figures are estimates meant to convey organizational and financial impact, that no rights can be derived from them, and adds a tolerance band (he noted roughly +/- 10-15%).
- License costs are flagged as "to discuss internally" rather than presented as fixed.
- Where a real assessment is still needed (e.g. a network readiness assessment before voice/video), he says so instead of estimating blind.

The effect is a report a manager can read top to bottom and, at any section, know what it concludes, what is still open, and what it will cost in plan-hours and implement-hours.

*Bron: geanonimiseerde aanpak uit een Salomon-IT klantopdracht (a shared-service organization for municipal real estate and sports facilities (joint venture with municipalities)).*
