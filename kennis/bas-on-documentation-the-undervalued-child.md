# Bas on documentation: the 'undervalued child' of IT

Bas opens his documentation section with characteristic sarcasm: "Welke IT'er is er niet gek op?!" If there's one thing he has learned over the years, it's that documentation turns out to be an 'undervalued child' (ondergeschoven kindje): nobody wants to do it, it eats time, and its usefulness and necessity are badly underestimated.

He argues documentation plays an indispensable role in the adoption phase (though you start earlier). His reasons it matters:
- **Continuity and knowledge retention** even when team members leave or change roles.
- **Daily management and maintenance**: documented processes (monitoring, incident handling, maintenance) give teams clear guidance, reducing errors and miscommunication.
- **Governance and compliance**: precisely recording procedures, roles and responsibilities supports compliance with rules like AVG/GDPR, ISO norms or industry standards, and makes audits easier.
- **Scaling and onboarding**: well-documented decisions help scale and get new team members 'up to speed' faster.
- **Incident resolution**: with documentation on configurations, network topologies and architecture, teams find root causes faster, shortening response time and downtime. Bas reframes incidents as inevitable: it's not a question of *whether* something will go wrong, but *when*.
- **Training and knowledge transfer**, and acting as a **feedback mechanism** under PDCA.

He punctuates the section with the maxim "If it's not documented, it didn't happen."
