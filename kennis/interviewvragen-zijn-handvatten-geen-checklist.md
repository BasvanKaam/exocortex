# Interview questions are handholds, not a checklist

When I hand an intake question set to a client's internal lead (or use one myself), the questions are meant to give handholds for a conversation, not to be read out and ticked off one by one. The aim is a good joint result, not full coverage of a list. I say this explicitly when I pass the questions over.

## The principles
- **Conversation over recitation.** Reading questions verbatim kills the dialogue. The list is there so the interviewer never runs dry and stays on the themes, not so every item gets asked.
- **Reusable, not rigid.** I tell the lead they can reuse and reshape items as needed. A subset, reordered and reworded, is fine.
- **Audience determines framing.** End-user questions and management questions cover overlapping ground but are phrased differently. Don't reuse a user-facing set verbatim with management; reframe for what that audience cares about.
- **Warm up before you drill down.** Lead with an open, aspirational question ("what does your ideal workplace look like?") before any structured round, so people talk freely first.

## Why I hold this
Structured surveys feel safe but produce thin, defensive answers. The richest signal (the real irritations, the workarounds, the wait that everyone has normalized) comes out when the respondent is talking, not answering. The question set is scaffolding for that conversation.
