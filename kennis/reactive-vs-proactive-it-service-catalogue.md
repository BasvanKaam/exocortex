# Reactive vs. Proactive IT and the Service Catalogue

**Reactive IT:** the business asks for X or Y and the internal or external IT organization scrambles to make it happen after the fact.

**Proactive IT:** IT works with service/product/service catalogues. It anticipates and predicts what will be needed, or proactively suggests, while keeping previously agreed specifications, SLAs and other service/quality agreements in place, so the catalogue stays in line with the business's expectations, objectives, wishes and requirements. The hallmark is that once a service or product is chosen it is available for use within a short time, because it already exists and does not need to be conceived or built first ('click and ready', klik en klaar).

**Why cloud enables the shift:** moving from reactive to proactive normally means investing up front in money and time, which smaller IT organizations (especially SMB) often lack. A cloud provider offers near-inexhaustible scalability no single company can match, so the 'click and ready' principle becomes the rule rather than the exception, and the sheer quantity of services means many get cheaper over time (varies per service/provider). The shift from a CapEx model (invest up front) to an OpEx model (pay for what you use/consume) is central; it requires a different mindset but lets both small and large IT organizations be seen as internal IT service providers.

Source: Bas van Kaam, Salomon IT blog, 2019-09-17.
