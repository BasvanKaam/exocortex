# Virtual usually beats physical, but leasing changes the Moonshot math

In June 2015 Bas published a position he had been sitting on for weeks (he had written an earlier, harsher comparison and deliberately not published it, glad he waited). His stance: in most cases virtual comes before physical because virtual is more flexible (spin up more VMs on demand, repurpose spare compute, run all workloads on one platform, use MCS instead of WDS+PVS, easier and more familiar setup). He pushed back on Moonshot's marketing claims: getting rid of hypervisor complexity "doesn't cut it" because the industry has sized hypervisors well for a decade and they are a commodity; Moonshot setup (PXE, firmware, cabling, WDS, HP chassis management) is not actually simpler; and power-savings claims looked marginal versus hyper-converged appliances. BUT his key evolution: if you can lease a Moonshot chassis + cartridges at a low monthly fee, the per-desktop cost drops to equal-or-cheaper than virtual while gaining flexibility (no buying 15 cartridges at once, HP mounts and cables it, 4-hour onsite SLA, future cartridge upgrades included). His verdict: "Pick your poison." In his own comment replies he was more skeptical still, calling Moonshot effectively a "1.0 product" that "needs to become more flexible" and saying "in its current form I don't see a bright future ahead."
