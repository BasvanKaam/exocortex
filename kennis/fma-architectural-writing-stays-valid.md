# Why architectural writing outlasts feature-by-feature writing

A durable lesson framed through a dated product: when Bas started his 'Inside Citrix' book (~late 2015), Citrix had just announced the quarterly release cycle for XenApp and XenDesktop. Realising he could never keep pace with features shipping every three months, he deliberately wrote about the FlexCast Management Architecture (FMA) and closely related tech from an architectural point of view rather than as an install/config guide, and left the cloud portfolio out of scope.

The payoff: two years later he judged the book still ~75% valid for on-premises XA/XD, because architecture changes far slower than features. He also notes that from a development perspective little had changed in the FMA and its services, some services were added or activated but nothing genuinely new was being built, because Citrix had shifted focus to cloud. The transferable principle: write at the architecture/concept altitude when the product surface churns fast, it ages far better. (FMA, XA/XD 7.18, Server 2008 R2 era, all now historical.)
