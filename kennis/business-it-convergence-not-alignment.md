# Convergence, Not Alignment, Between Business and IT

Bas argues that the usual goal of "business/IT alignment" is not enough. He wants **convergence**: not two departments kept in sync, but the steps and the people brought together until the two effectively become one. Concretely he advocates exchanging people between departments so business and IT genuinely understand each other, rather than coordinating across a wall.

He is explicit about the gap this is meant to close. The classic failure pattern: business and IT are separate departments that do not collaborate, do not understand each other, are not on the same line, do not know what the other does or needs, do not consult enough, use their own jargon and do not share knowledge. Business decisions get made without involving IT (or are made non-SMART).

He captures the mutual resentment to make the point stick. Business about IT: "they take too long, they do not understand what we need, too expensive, they say no too often, they just do whatever." IT about business: "they keep changing their minds, they do not get what we do, they invent things without asking our opinion, they go around us to external IT services."

His position: IT must become (or already be) a strategic, innovative partner of the business, equal in standing, each side complementing the other. Business sets direction (wishes, requirements, priorities, opportunities); IT makes it possible (goals and IT standards, innovation and automation, governance and processes, skills and training). The shared discipline is to involve, listen, ask, learn and do it together. IT should also be able to express ROI. He frames this collaboration point as "where the magic happens."
