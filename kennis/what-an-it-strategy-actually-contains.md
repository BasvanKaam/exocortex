# What an IT strategy actually contains

Bas defines a strategic IT plan as a detailed document covering multiple facets of the organization, going well beyond technology choices. It weighs and accounts for: risks, cost forecasts, budgets, investments already made, running projects, strengths and weaknesses (SWOT), how the current IT and other departments look and operate (RACI), stated goals plus approach, and more.

The core principle: technology plays a supporting role and is never a goal in itself, and this holds for every conceivable cloud solution too. A strategic IT plan describes how technology supports, strengthens or perhaps changes the organizational and business-driven needs, not the reverse.

Keep it readable: he applies KISS (Keep It Stupid Simple). Nobody wants a 100+ page document full of jargon. Be clear, don't dodge.

**Roadmapping** is how strategy reaches execution. As part of the broader IT strategy you assess the current technological state and form a picture of where IT should/wants to grow over the next ~2 to 3 years. Attaching a detailed roadmap gives overview via timelines, costs and deliverables, builds buy-in through substantiation, and grows IT and "the business" closer together. It is the bridge between strategy/vision and actual execution, bringing the "stip op de horizon" closer.
