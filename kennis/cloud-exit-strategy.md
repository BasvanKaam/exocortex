# Cloud exit strategy: why and how

Bas's recurring argument: a cloud exit strategy is like an expensive car insurance — you'd rather not take it out, but when something goes wrong you're glad you have it. His sharper framing: the exit comes before the onboarding.

Reasons (triggers) to exit a cloud provider that he lists:
- The service disappoints: poor service / support.
- SLAs are not honored; terms and conditions change.
- The provider unexpectedly discontinues certain services.
- Data is no longer stored within the Netherlands.
- Too expensive, or prices are continuously adjusted.
- The provider quits or goes bankrupt.
- The grass is (or seems) greener elsewhere.
- A change of policy / strategy.
- Multi-cloud, or moving back on-prem.

Exit framework phases he draws: Preparation -> Inventory -> Specific -> Triggers -> Execution -> Follow-up.

Key concerns it protects against: data residency (Netherlands), price volatility, provider failure, and SLA erosion.
