# Involve and educate users early or adoption fails

From Bas's Nerdio era, an AVD-specific take on a theme he returns to often: educating and involving users is one of the main factors determining whether adoption of new technology succeeds.

His argument: people stick to what they know and see change as scary ("we've always done it like this, it works great, why change?"). The fix is to guide and educate users about what's coming and, crucially, *why* the company chose this route (retiring legacy, becoming more future-proof, secure, eventually easier) — and to focus on what's in it for them. The sooner you involve users, the higher the acceptance rate: if you build a platform for months in isolation and then drop it on people overnight, adoption will be low. When users are asked for their opinion early they feel ownership, which is partly true. Examining the current platform and users' daily routines should be part of any preparation phase — it's where you find the most important questions about what to improve. He is careful to say there is no single golden approach since every company differs, but early user involvement is crucial to any successful virtual desktop implementation.
