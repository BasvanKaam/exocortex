# Why have a cloud exit strategy - the exit comes before onboarding

Bas's framing of the cloud exit strategy (Chapter 12): you don't assume you'll need it, but you must account for it - like an insurance policy you take out. It is realistic to expect that one day you'll want at least part of your applications, data and systems hosted elsewhere, and then you'll be glad you addressed it early. His signature line: 'De exit komt voor de onboarding!' - preparations for a possible exit begin before implementation.

An exit strategy prepares an organisation to leave its current cloud environment - to switch providers, return on-premises, or go hybrid. Drivers he lists: costs running out of control as usage grows or as the provider raises prices; vendor lock-in (each provider uses its own technologies and standards, complicating migration); and compliance - new laws may require data in certain regions or a provider may no longer meet compliance requirements, and an exit strategy lets you respond to legal changes relatively fast without endangering operations.

Other benefits: protection of data and applications via a solid migration plan (minimising data loss); strategic autonomy to respond to technological or market change without being tied to one vendor; and safeguarding business continuity by anticipating risks like security incidents, bankruptcies or other critical problems at a provider. A well-designed strategy covers data migration, application portability, security measures, a clear timeline and a set of actions.
