# Bas on disaster recovery: no blueprint, and don't forget the BUDGET

Bas has written disaster recovery (DR) plans for various clients - a sizeable job, and completely different every time. Some concepts recur at every organisation, but the architectures and products used vary widely. The way they handled back-ups, restores (and testing them), high availability and so on differed almost every time, so there is no true blueprint.

His two emphatic warnings:
1. Budget is the aspect most often forgotten. People quickly demand a DR plan and strategy - until the financial impact becomes clear.
2. Testing is non-negotiable: a back-up of data or a system that you're not sure can actually be restored is worthless. You can build a beautiful plan, but if you don't know it works, it's useless. Disasters are rare and most organisations thankfully never face one, but Ransomware is an increasingly frequent threat. Back-up and restore are also used in everyday situations, e.g. when someone loses a file.

He also notes DR is mandatory for many sectors (financial, healthcare, critical infrastructure, government, defence, etc.) - more than you'd think - and that this is not cloud-specific.
