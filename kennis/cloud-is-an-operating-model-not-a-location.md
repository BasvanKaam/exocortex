# Cloud Is an Operating Model, Not a Location

What distinguishes cloud from the pre-cloud era of using someone else's computers is the service-oriented model built around it: easily scaling resources up and down, paying only for what you use, and switching off what you no longer need. Provisioning server and desktop systems with their services is trivially easy (IaaS, PaaS, DaaS, SaaS), production-ready in a few clicks without worrying about maintenance and monitoring.

The key insight: 'cloud-born' services increasingly become available inside your own datacenter. This lets a client use the cloud operating model and build a bridge from their own datacenter (private cloud) to the public cloud, without having to move a large part of their infrastructure, data, or application landscape to the cloud.

What ultimately matters is the (operational) model, not where your systems, data, or applications end up. Under the right circumstances a 100% cloud-based environment can be an end station, but reaching it requires a focus on the on-premises environment first, both your own and in general.
