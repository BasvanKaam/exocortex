# Position: phase the rollout and keep part of the legacy estate intact

When Bas designs the migration path in an advisory report, his default is a **phased rollout that deliberately leaves part of the existing infrastructure in place**, rather than changing everything at once. His stated reasoning, in his own framing:

- **"We don't want to change too much at once for the end users - that benefits adoption."** The constraint that drives the sequencing is human, not technical.
- **Leaving part of the current (data) infrastructure intact lets you move faster.** Migrating heavier or contentious workloads can happen gradually and in parallel, while a definitive solution is worked out in the background.
- **Where a legacy system is disliked or barely used, don't pour effort into rebuilding it 1:1 in the new world.** In this engagement the old collaboration platform was a source of complaints, so instead of faithfully re-creating it online he proposed integrating the useful parts into the new tooling and letting the old platform slowly fade out. A like-for-like migration is not always possible or worth it.

His sequencing also bakes in change management: open questions are answered with the client first, an adoption plan is written, ambassadors are named, preparatory training and a kick-off happen before the technical waves, and **every phase is followed by a test-and-aftercare period (he budgets ~2 weeks).** Lower-risk, low-visibility moves (mail to cloud, profile containers, office suite without the collaboration app) go first; the highest-impact change for users goes last.

The principle generalizes beyond any one platform: sequence by user impact, protect adoption by changing less at a time, and don't sink effort into faithfully recreating things the organization was already unhappy with.
