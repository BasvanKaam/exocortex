# What actually slows cloud adoption (and why cloud-first beats cloud-only)

Bas's structured case for why full cloud-only migration stays out of reach for most organizations, and why a step-by-step cloud-first or hybrid approach is the realistic route. Distilled from his 2019 Cloud Masterclass essay, re-read in 2025 and still largely holding up.

## The blockers he names
- **Traditional Windows applications.** Very few orgs can run on SaaS alone. Classic Windows apps remain part of the picture for years and often depend on other systems and software.
- **Latency sensitivity.** Some workloads can't tolerate the round-trip to a distant region.
- **Data residency / control.** Some orgs are unable or unwilling to move certain data types to the cloud. Loss of control can be a decisive, deal-breaking issue (the human factor).
- **Knowledge, skills and governance gaps.** A lack of in-house skills and proper oversight is a major challenge for many companies.
- **Scalability and availability limits.** Power shortages and outages hitting major cloud services made headlines; one reason hyperscalers now push on-premises cloud offerings.

These developments slow adoption, drive reversals back to on-prem, and sometimes block cloud initiatives entirely.

## The frame to evaluate cloud against
- Cloud's core traits (NIST, 2011): on-demand self-service, broad network access, resource pooling, rapid elasticity, measured service. Little changed since.
- The marketed promises to interrogate: lower cost, improved security, instant availability, ease of use, infinite scalability, access from anywhere, always available, open standards, hands-off management, continuous innovation.

## How Bas pressure-tests the promises
- **Cheaper? Not necessarily.** Pay-per-use often gets more expensive over long horizons. Multi-year commits (1/3/5 yr) bring discounts. Don't forget one-off and temporary transition/migration costs. Cloud is framed as Opex but there's more to it, and orgs realize this too late. The real value lives elsewhere: shorter time-to-market, immediate availability, less operational overhead, scalability, temporary use, flexibility, which then drive savings in other areas.
- **More secure? Agreed.** Few orgs can invest hundreds of millions a year in security; the hyperscalers can.
- **Instantly available? True, with caveats.** Depends on service type and scale; spinning up a thousand VMs still takes time, coordinate with your provider in advance.
- **Easy to use? Not entirely.** Depends heavily on the service.

## The takeaway
Use cloud services where they make sense and add real value. No added value, no reason to move. Step by step is the only approach that makes sense. "Cloud only? No. Cloud first or hybrid? Yes. The only way forward."
