# Citrix origins: from Citrus Systems to WinFrame, and the birth of ICA

Durable history (sourced partly from his own book Inside Citrix). Citrix was founded in 1989 by Edward Iacobucci, a former IBM developer, first in Richardson, Texas, then relocated to Coral Springs, Florida. It started as 'Citrus Systems' but renamed to Citrix after another company claimed the Citrus trademark. Early focus was remote access for Microsoft's OS/2.

After two years it shipped Citrix Multiuser, an OS/2 extension, where the ICA (Independent Computing Architecture) protocol was born around 1990/1991. When Microsoft pivoted from OS/2 to Windows, Multiuser 1.0 became near-useless; investors (including Microsoft) kept Citrix alive, leading to Multiuser 2.0 / Multi-Win in 1992 (DOS-compatible, up to 5 simultaneous users). Citrix then licensed Windows NT 3.5 source from Microsoft and turned NT into a multi-user platform. WinView shipped in 1993 (DOS + Windows apps); WinFrame, a true NT-based multi-user OS allowing up to 15 simultaneous users, launched in 1995. In 1997 HQ moved to Fort Lauderdale and Mark Templeton became CEO (through 2015).
