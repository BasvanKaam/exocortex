# 'Keeping the lights on' starves innovation; offload commodity to fix it

> Referentie uit het Weolcan Cloud Governance Framework (WCGF). Niet Bas zijn eigen IP, hier bewaard als naslag.

the framework's central argument behind the placement model is an imbalance of resources. Disentangling the application landscape reveals the main cause of slow innovation: 70 to 80 percent of resources are spent on Commodity Applications, also called 'Keeping the lights on'. That leaves only 20 to 30 percent for Competitive Applications. This is exactly why market newcomers, who exclusively build fun, new, innovative services, enjoy a competitive advantage; they carry no commodity ballast.

His prescribed correction: move legacy applications out of the Manage Scale phase and accelerate them to the Offload phase (typically SaaS). That saves time and money, which is then redirected to the competitive half of the application landscape. The placement quadrant is therefore not a cataloguing exercise but a tool for rebalancing spend toward differentiation.

This sits inside his broader stance that a cloud strategy should focus on accelerating innovation, because to stay competitive companies must innovate at high speed and software is increasingly how value is created, not merely on cutting cost.
