# Key Takeaways Chapter 10 (Cloud Security)

Bas's chapter-10 takeaways on Cloud security:
- Large Cloud providers (Microsoft, Amazon, Google) invest billions annually in security to protect customer data, meet strict regulation and repel cyberattacks. These economies of scale deliver security standards often unreachable for individual companies.
- Robustness is reinforced by a broad range of certifications (ISO 27001, SOC 2, GDPR, HIPAA), letting them serve customers across sectors worldwide.
- Providers regularly work with partners such as Check Point to test and improve security, running studies and finding vulnerabilities.
- Zero Trust ('nooit vertrouwen, altijd verifiëren') is a key modern concept; access is only granted to authorised users/devices regardless of location, spanning all network layers from outer shell to the core of data and applications.
- Threats keep evolving: ransomware, phishing and zero-day exploits are currently the biggest risks, often succeeding through human error (clicking untrustworthy links, weak passwords). Tomorrow's threats are more advanced: AI-driven attacks, targeted ransomware, deepfakes, and future quantum-computing-based threats.
- A detailed risk analysis helps identify and prioritise risks. But security is not only technical; it needs a human-centred approach with regular training and awareness.
- Avoid assumptions: unclear procedures and poor communication create needless risk. Security must be an integral part of organisational culture, not just a technical question. Stay vigilant.
