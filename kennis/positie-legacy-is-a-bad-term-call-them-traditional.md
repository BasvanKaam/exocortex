# Stop calling them 'legacy' apps, call them 'traditional'

Bas takes a stance against the term 'legacy applications'. His argument: at most companies these applications are critical to the business process, so labelling them 'legacy' is not appreciated. He prefers 'traditional applications', especially given that new Windows applications are still being created every day, more Windows apps are added than retired. The point is both rhetorical (respect what the business depends on) and factual (the Windows app estate is still growing, not shrinking).
