# Tim Berners-Lee and the World Wide Web

In the 1980s-90s the foundation for today's Cloud computing was laid by the development of the World Wide Web. British scientist Tim Berners-Lee, working at CERN in Switzerland, sought a more efficient way to share research information. In 1989 he published a proposal for a large hypertext system of linked information, which became the World Wide Web.

In 1990 he built the first web browser, called WorldWideWeb, which ran on NeXT computers (the company Steve Jobs founded after leaving Apple). In 1991 the first website launched on CERN's network. The arrival of browsers like Mosaic in 1993 made the web accessible to ordinary users, triggering an explosion of online activity. Mosaic was the first browser available across multiple operating systems (Windows and Mac OS) and inspired the first commercial browsers Netscape Navigator and Microsoft Internet Explorer.
