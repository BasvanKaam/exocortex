# Cloud adoption framework: four phased pillars

My default structure for guiding an organization to the cloud. Four pillars run in sequence and partly overlap. The point is structure and clarity: everyone knows where they stand and the chance of missing something drops. Pragmatic and practice-oriented, not theory.

## The four pillars

1. **Strategy** — the why and the high-level direction. Skip or shorten this when the client already made the cloud decision and set direction themselves; don't re-litigate a settled strategy.
2. **Insight** — the most important pillar. Inventory and quantify the application, system, data, network and security landscape including all dependencies. Make a first cut on priority/impact, what moves to the cloud and in what form. Surface risks and knowledge gaps. Write a first financial business case. Ends in a go/no-go.
3. **Execution** — finalize rationalization decisions per component, write the technical/functional design, build/migrate the first workloads against an approved migration and implementation plan, adapt governance and processes, arrange skills/readiness.
4. **Adoption** — finish and take into production the work started in Execution, handle the wave-3 exceptions, hand over to daily operations (decharge). End state is a fully operational (often hybrid) cloud platform plus a final report with lessons learned.

## Working principles across the pillars
- Per completed pillar I can deliver a short report (often a single page) or a presentation, especially for management.
- We do it together; client involvement can be as large or small as they want. Often a single workshop gives me enough to proceed largely independently.
- Transparent by default: any question at any moment, and the client is free to shadow the work to pick up knowledge.
- A final report with lessons learned closes the engagement.
