# The 80/20 'two kinds of sysadmin' framework

Bas's reusable mental model for IT pros (acknowledging there are people in between):

- The tech-savvy 'I really like to dig in' sysadmin: deep-dives whenever possible, blogs, helps on forums and social media, contributes to community programs. Work becomes hobby and vice versa. Not for everyone, and that's fine.
- The all-round, laid-back 'I don't want to spend my free time getting to know new technology' sysadmin: stays current, keeps certifications up to date, doesn't mind digging in a bit, but won't become a PowerShell/scripting/automation guru. Loves the job, finds fulfillment, but draws a line. Also fine.

His estimate: the ratio is roughly 80/20 in favor of the less tech-savvy administrator. He uses this split as the foundation for arguing that management tooling (WVD/AVD or otherwise) should not require 'special' skills or a long learning curve, and that good tooling should reduce time to market and help make money. The same 20% rule applies inside a company: most firms have only a couple of senior tech-savvy admins carrying the code, templates and automation.
