# Method: building a company into a knowledge organisation (Detron case)

His own three-year case study in turning a company into a knowledge organisation, and the proof points behind it.

Levers and results:
- The Detron Bloggers Borrel grew active bloggers company-wide from 2-3 to 12. His verdict: 'Betere reclame kun je, je als bedrijf niet wensen.'
- More colleagues attending and presenting at conferences, in the Netherlands and abroad, putting the Detron name and its professionals in the spotlight.
- Community recognition: from zero community awards in mid-2014 to five colleagues holding titles like Citrix CTP/CTA, VMware vExpert, Nutanix NTC, and RES RSVP.
- Internal knowledge sharing run by business developers, each owning a work domain (Connectivity, Security, Cloud/On-premises, Workspace), with sessions of 50-75 people down to weekend solder sessions still drawing 20+.
- Regular informal brainstorm sessions (pizza and a beer) to think through how to respond to recent technology developments, with security a recent example.
- Process certification alongside the tech: dozens certified in PRINCE2, ITIL V3, and Scrum Foundation via organised workshops.
- A Detron Technology Board acting as sounding board and feeding the Detron ICT Kennis Cafe (DiK).

The through-line: giving knowledge away freely inside the community and around the company name does not go unnoticed, and it compounds into employer brand.
