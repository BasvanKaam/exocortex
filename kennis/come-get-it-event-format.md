# Come Get IT event: Bas's community evening format

Come Get IT is the recurring in-person community event Bas runs under Cloud Generation IT (CGIT). The format is an evening with a small, curated line-up of invited speakers, each delivering a focused technical session (typically 35-40 minutes), centred on end-user computing, cloud and Microsoft technology. There is also an associated Come Get IT Podcast.

The event template (one edition at Landgoed Salentein, Nijkerk):

- Reception with a drink, then a shared dinner, then the well-known networking drinks to let people get to know each other.
- A small, curated set of talks. One edition pivoted from three to two sessions: one technically substantive, one technique-adjacent.
- Deliberate balance of soft and hard content: a trainer/inspirator running a practical mini-workshop (Rob Tummers, 2enable) alongside a senior solution architect doing a technical deep-dive (Jits Langedijk, NVIDIA, on deep learning, VR, vGPU and GRID), with the explicit promise to skip the marketing talk.
- A premium, atmospheric venue is treated as part of the draw ('die is wederom uitstekend (understatement)').
- Attendees are encouraged to bring a colleague, contact, customer or other interested person, feeding the community flywheel.

How Bas curates it:
- He invites recognised practitioners (vendor engineers, MVPs, authors, community figures), not marketers. He explicitly steers sessions away from marketing talk toward technical substance ("de marketing praat even laten voor wat het is").
- He vouches personally for speakers he knows ("iets wat ik persoonlijk (Bas) kan beamen").
- Capacity is limited and seats fill fast; he promotes via short, named speaker-spotlight blog posts ("Uitgelicht!") in the run-up.
- Guests, colleagues, clients and other interested parties are all welcome (open community spirit).

Observed editions and themes:
- 28 November edition: GPU / AI / VR theme (NVIDIA).
- 2 April edition: Microsoft theme (Teams, WVD, security).
- 4 July edition: virtualization / HCI theme (Nutanix, VMware), with an F1 angle.

The through-line: content must connect one-to-one to the attendees' daily work as IT specialists, and marketing is consciously kept out of the technical session.
