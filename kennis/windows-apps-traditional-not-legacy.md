# Windows apps are 'traditional', not 'legacy'

A recurring Bas position, stated here plainly (June 2018): he refuses to call Windows applications 'legacy', insisting on 'traditional' instead. 'I get why people call Windows applications legacy, I just don't fully agree.' His evidence: ~75% of all applications are still Windows applications, and in the survey their use actually went up again. He concedes new types (SaaS, mobile, universal) will slowly erode that share 'in the years to come', but rejects the idea that SaaS replaces them soon.

This ties directly to his app-layering argument: 30-40% of Windows apps cannot be (or aren't) virtualized, and most won't have a direct SaaS alternative, so abstraction layers remain valuable. The terminology stance ('not legacy') is a small but consistent rhetorical flag of his throughout this era.
