# Bas's practical blogging playbook (process and craft rules)

An internal memo Bas wrote to colleagues, capturing how he actually produces blog posts. It is craft-level, not motivational, and complements his 'learn in public' philosophy with concrete mechanics.

**Length.** Aim for at least ~400 words; for the technical posts on comegetit.nl longer is fine, but cap it around 1500-1600 words.

**Peer review in Word.** Write in Word (or OneNote) with track changes on, not directly in WordPress, because WordPress can lose work on save. Circulate a first draft and give everyone a day or two to react; there is always someone with a moment. A draft is a draft, so don't spend hours finetuning before review. Constructive criticism only, no nitpicking for its own sake.

**Let it rest.** Leave a draft one or two days before sending it for review, then reread it: you spot errors and gain new insights, and the post gets better.

**Just start, write in stages.** Begin even when you don't know the topic, the how, or the angle; it comes once you start. Topics are never the bottleneck; even covered topics are fine to redo in your own way, because readers come for your style, angle and experience. If getting going is hard, write 10-15 minutes a day and the post is done by the end of the week. He dumps what's already in his head straight down, order be damned, sketches chapter headings with a few self-only sentences, and fleshes them out later so nothing is forgotten.

**Write from experience.** Readers value writing rooted in your own experience; lean on it, refer to it, give an opinion. Substantiation (onderbouwing) is the single most important thing.

**Capture ideas on the phone.** Use the notes app for fleeting ideas (a 'kattebel'), often at conferences; if you think you'll remember it later, you usually won't.

**Formatting and readability.** Keep paragraphs to ~4-5 sentences (2-3 reads best). One block quote per post (two max), use italics to make a word or phrase stand out, prefer high-resolution images (low-res looks grainy when shared on LinkedIn), and standardise heading styles across the team.

**SEO and security, lightly.** Don't go blind on SEO plugins; fix the low-hanging fruit and move on. Log into WordPress weekly to check for updates, install new plugins only by agreement ('minder = beter'), and use two-factor everywhere.

**Distribution.** Best publish slot: Tuesday morning 10-11am (from his own stats). Never publish on Friday or the weekend, but do write then; even writing to throw away is never wasted, because the more you write the more you learn. LinkedIn is the primary platform (Twitter too weak in NL); share with a direct link to the LinkedIn post, and make sure the team knows when something publishes.
