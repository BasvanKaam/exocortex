# Bas's Lessons Learned document: a living continuous-learning tool

Bas recommends keeping a Lessons Learned document during the execution and adoption phases as a powerful way to capture useful experiences and insights, for both the current project and future initiatives (you'll likely encounter new Cloud use-cases).

Key points of his method:
- It is **not a static report but a dynamic instrument** capturing experiences, challenges, solutions and recommendations. Simply put: what is/was going well, what is/was going wrong, across everything from project management and technical issues to team collaboration and stakeholder interaction.
- **Review regularly**, e.g. after each project phase or at the end of a sprint. Encourage team members to note insights the moment they hit something, so information isn't lost.
- **Keep a fixed structure** for usability, e.g. categorising insights per phase or theme: name, date, sub-project, observation.
- It is used in practice to optimise processes, help onboard new members (who avoid hitting the same problems), and aid risk management by learning from earlier mistakes.
- Above all it builds a **culture of continuous learning** centred on reflection and improvement, Plan-Do-Check-Act, growing the knowledge of the whole organisation, not just the team.

He closes with Henry Ford: "The only real mistake is the one from which we learn nothing."
