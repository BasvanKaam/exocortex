# Bas on digital nomads: living while you work

Bas opens Chapter 16 noting that across ten years working for everything from government to smaller commercial firms, he saw digital nomads grow enormously popular, especially in the commercial sector and mostly in the more creative professions.

His characterisation: digital nomads are a peculiar breed who combine work and life on their own terms, throw out traditional ideas, and refuse to be tied down by an office job or fixed home. For them it's not about a fat lease car, the Friday-afternoon drinks or a "permanent contract with security" — it's about freedom. Work from wherever you want, whenever you want. Not "working to live," but living while you work.

Many are freelancers or entrepreneurs who write, design, code, coach or train — needing only a laptop and a reasonably stable internet connection, from a beach tent in Bali, a coffee shop in Lisbon, or a camper somewhere in Southern Europe. Some move place to place, others settle longer in one location. Not because they have to, but because they can.

Bas notes the lifestyle is growing among older generations too (fifties and sixties leaving corporate). His own eldest daughter (22) and her boyfriend are an example: why immediately tie yourself to a rental, a car and a 9-to-5 when there's so much to discover? Each to their own.
