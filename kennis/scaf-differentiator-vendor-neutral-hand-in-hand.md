# What Makes SCAF Different: Vendor-Neutral and Hand-in-Hand

In the handbook Bas states plainly what sets the SCAF apart from other frameworks. First, it is fully cloud-vendor neutral: Salomon is in no way bound to Microsoft, Google, AWS or anyone else, knowledge of all platforms is present, and the only goal is the advice that best fits the specific client case. He argues this neutrality, combined with deep IT-Roadmap expertise, is a unique combination.

Second, he insists technology choices and organizational change go hand in hand: business objectives and specific wishes/requirements drive the work, and the alignment between IT and management plays a leading role. He calls out that people often forget these two sides of a successful IT organization are tightly interlinked. Cultural shifts are treated as a consequence, never a goal in themselves, but still given attention. He also commits the framework to KISS (Keep It Stupid Simple) and "to the point" writing, and frames the whole thing as dynamic, not static, so the team can respond to events that demand a change in approach, a product, or even the broader vision.

This is his governance philosophy in miniature: independent advice, business-before-technology, and pragmatism over dogma.
