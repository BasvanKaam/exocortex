# Early user involvement makes or breaks adoption

Bas argues that educating and involving users early is the single most decisive factor in a successful virtual-desktop migration. People stick to what they know and treat change as threatening ('we've always done it like this, why change?'). If you build a platform in isolation for months and then drop it on users as a fait accompli, adoption will be low. Involving users early, asking for their feedback, and framing the why and the what's-in-it-for-them turns resistance into buy-in. The act of examining the current platform and how users actually work should be part of every preparation phase regardless. There is no golden one-size-fits-all approach, but early involvement in some form is non-negotiable.
