# SCAF Cloud governance: putting the dots on the i

Governance is wide-ranging. In Bas's SCAF, it was begun in the execution phase and during adoption you 'put the dots on the i'. He stresses governance needs time to grow and will always be a process subject to change.

Assuming governance (small or large) is described and applied in practice, the adoption phase does another control pass and makes changes where needed: state the current situation, what is planned, where you ran into trouble, and any other suggestions. As part of the governance model Bas wants these things addressed:
- All Cloud resources and their costs are visible, with someone/a team responsible and tooling in use; a cost-aware organisation is the ultimate goal.
- Agile working in iterations is applied in practice.
- The main processes (incident, problem, change management) are set up and operational, however minimal.
- All teams are inducted, roles and responsibilities clear.
- New products/services are taken into management, with daily operations covering monitoring, alerting, patching, reporting, automation, lifecycle and more.
- A strict security policy is applied, enforced and monitored.
- Ambassadors go into the field to promote, run workshops/trainings and gather questions/feedback.
- A communication plan (possibly from the ambassadors) is drawn up to regularly communicate which phase the Cloud adoption is in.
