# Position: force the old way out, but only after early involvement and training

Bas treats adoption as the make-or-break of a workspace change, and he holds a specific, slightly contrarian position on it: **the old way of working should be made impossible** once the new one is in place. People will default back to the familiar otherwise, and a half-migrated organization keeps its sprawl of storage locations, communication channels and shadow IT - exactly what the client wanted to escape.

But the force is conditional. He is explicit that you may only "force" people onto the new tooling **if enough time and attention go into teaching and guiding them.** The two halves are inseparable. His adoption recipe around the hard cut-over:

- **Involve employees early.** Explain why we're doing this, how it helps the organization, and above all the benefits - because "what you don't know, you don't miss," so the value has to be made visible.
- **Find ambassadors** within the organization and lean on them.
- **Communicate continuously** - regular interim status updates, room to ask questions and voice concerns, walk-in information mornings/afternoons.
- **Offer multiple training formats** and temporary on-site support, especially the first week or two. Classroom groups of ~15-20 work best for interaction.
- **Accept that not everyone will be enthusiastic** and that getting everyone on board takes time.
- **Define persona groups** (willingness and skill level differ per employee) so training and guidance fit, without spending weeks on it - the client usually has the best view of who is who.

The through-line: a hard cut-over is justified, but only as the back half of a deal whose front half is early involvement, clear benefits, ambassadors and real training. Force without support is just frustration.

*Bron: geanonimiseerde aanpak uit een Salomon-IT klantopdracht (a shared-service organization for municipal real estate and sports facilities (joint venture with municipalities)).*
