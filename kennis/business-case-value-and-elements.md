# Why Write a Business Case, and Its Key Elements

Bas: a well-founded business case matters for every IT or cloud project. It substantiates the project's necessity (costs, benefits, risks), engages stakeholders for support/draagvlak, enables data-based decisions, allocates resources better (budgets, resources, timelines), and helps teams take ownership. Many projects fail, overrun, or cost too much because there's no properly worked-out business case underneath. A strong business case revolves around the **why, what, how and who** of your project and serves as a reference throughout the whole lifecycle, not just at the start.

Key elements (optional — pick what's relevant per project): 1) Executive Summary; 2) Project definition (SCAF helps); 3) Vision, goals and objectives (SCAF strategy phase); 4) Project scope — what's in/out, to prevent **scope creep** (new tasks/requirements added without adjusting time, budget and resources); 5) Background info (market research, strategy fit — optional/possibly redundant); 6) Success criteria and stakeholder requirements; 7) Project plan with a **work breakdown structure (WBS)** — task hierarchy; 8) Project budget (see Chapter 8); 9) Planning/timelines, preferably visualised in a **Gantt chart** (bar chart of tasks, milestones, timeline); 10) Project governance; 11) Communication plan (esp. Chapter 4, not to be underestimated); 12) Progress reports; 13) Financial evaluation (ROI, TCO, break-even); 14) Market analysis; 15) Competitor analysis (not needed for internal projects); 16) SWOT analysis (Chapter 5); 17) Marketing strategy (how the new workplace is internally positioned and 'sold'); 18) Risk analysis (Chapter 10).
