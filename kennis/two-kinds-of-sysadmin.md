# The two kinds of sysadmin

A framing Bas uses to explain why tooling and GUIs matter, and why automation isn't a universal answer.

Roughly two archetypes:

- **The tech-savvy "I really like to dig in" sysadmin.** Deep-dives whenever they can, blogs about it, helps on forums and social, contributes to community programs. Work becomes hobby, or vice versa. Maybe 20% of IT.
- **The all-round, laid-back "I don't want to spend my free time on new tech" sysadmin.** Doesn't mind digging in a bit and staying current, keeps certifications up to date, but has no interest in going all-in all the time. Prefers things automated for them and to-the-point solutions with easy GUIs. Will not become a PowerShell or scripting guru. This is ~80% of people in IT, give or take.

Both are fine. The point: a management solution should not require "special" skills, and should let the larger group be productive too, not just the automation gurus.
