# Hyper-V vs VMware in 2013: cheaper and simpler vs granular

How it worked then: Microsoft offered a single Hyper-V edition; the only real choice was System Center Datacenter vs Standard, which differed mainly in how many virtual OS environments you could manage (unlimited vs two), and both bundled two years of Software Assurance. VMware offered three vSphere editions plus essential/acceleration kits and separate Support-and-Subscription packages (Basic, Production) that you had to buy for at least a year per product.

The trade-off Bas drew out: Microsoft made the decision simple and was cheaper; VMware offered more granularity so you bought exactly what you needed. Replacing an existing hypervisor was rarely worth it (sunk cost, retraining time), but for greenfield deployments the functional gap was small enough that price often decided it. He noted statistics already showing Hyper-V winning new implementations especially at SMB/mid-market.
