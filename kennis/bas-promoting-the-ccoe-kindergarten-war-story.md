# Promoting the CCoE: the 'kindergarten' war story and strategies

Bas shares a war story: during a project (not even cloud-related, but that doesn't matter) he watched day in, day out how the CCoE was increasingly worked against. They came with ever more little rules and continuously checked everyone's work. For various individuals and teams it felt like being back in kindergarten ('alsof ze weer in de kleuterklas zaten'). His lesson: just like promoting the cloud project itself, the CCoE's role must be clearly communicated, with emphasis on its added value and benefits.

His strategies to promote the CCoE internally and make it visible and approachable:
- **Actively promote it:** use internal channels (blogs, internal wiki, company website, intranet) to share the CCoE's work, posting regular updates on projects and successes.
- **Spot training gaps and make a training plan:** identify missing knowledge/skills around cloud use and management and offer training.
- **Regular project reviews:** periodic evaluations of running projects involving the CCoE, keeping projects on schedule and sharing successes and lessons.
- **Define and measure KPIs:** e.g. number of running projects the CCoE is involved in; number of completed projects initiated/led by the CCoE; number of service-catalog items introduced by the CCoE; number of training sessions given or supported by the CCoE.
- **Share other performance metrics and successes:** e.g. process improvements or best-practice implementations.

Bas's marker of success: 'Succes is wanneer businessunits met het CCoE willen samenwerken. Hoezo Cloud? Wordt dan… waarom geen Cloud?' — success is when business units *want* to collaborate with the CCoE, and 'why cloud?' flips into 'why not cloud?'
