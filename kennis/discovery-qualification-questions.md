# Discovery and qualification: the questions I ask

My intake/qualification line for a cloud workplace fit. I start from the client's pain, not from the product, and I separate questions the client should hear from internal technical-qualification notes.

## Opening (client-facing)
- What are your current challenges? Is something specific going on right now?
- Have you heard of this solution? What's your reaction, have you tried anything, any experiences, concerns or objections?
- Are you satisfied with your current stack (e.g. the incumbent VDI/Citrix)? Looking for an alternative? Are costs a concern?
- On cautious interest: which licences do you already have, or plan to buy? (Entitlement drives both fit and price.)
- What could a next step look like? Book a session for more detail.

## Technical qualification (partly internal, not all for the client)
Fit is mostly a technical question. I get a high-level read on:
- What does the environment look like now? App types, where the data lives, VDI vs SBC vs a mix.
- Which Office / M365 licences are already present (entitlement check first).
- Can the client go cloud-only for the workplaces, or are there on-prem dependencies (apps, data, network, VPN, IaaS)?
- Are there specific incumbent features they can't live without (e.g. network optimisation, HDX, SD-WAN, specific policies)? If those are hard requirements, the simpler cloud option may not fit.
- Graphics workloads: everything except very heavy graphics tends to fit, but always test first.

## The honest-flagging habit
I keep an explicit list of **dependencies and points of attention** and I name them early. I'd rather surface a misfit in pre-sales than discover it in delivery. A useful rule of thumb I apply: in roughly 70%+ of cases the solution fits; a quick technical pre-sales pass tells you which bucket you're in. Don't oversell into the other 30%.
