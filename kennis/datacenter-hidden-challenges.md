# The less-obvious challenges of building datacenters

Under "Minder voor de hand liggend" Bas catalogues the under-discussed constraints on datacenter construction and operation:

- **Enormous energy demand** — in regions with limited reserves a stable supply is hard to guarantee, delaying builds and capping operational capacity; companies sometimes negotiate with local governments and utilities, raising cost.
- **Environmental impact** — stricter rules force compliance on energy efficiency, cooling and waste, lengthening permit procedures and raising build costs.
- **Cooling-water availability** — water-cooled servers struggle in water-scarce areas, pushing firms to costlier alternatives (air cooling, closed water circuits).
- **Building-material cost and availability** — supply-chain disruptions and demand for steel, copper, semiconductors and concrete drive shortages, cost and delays.
- **Shortage of specialised labour** — engineers, technicians, security specialists are scarce in some regions, raising build time and reliance on expensive external providers.
- **Security** — datacenters are targets for cyber and physical attacks, requiring access control, surveillance and cybersecurity investment.
- **Green-tech pressure** — renewables and efficient cooling can lower long-term cost but demand bigger upfront investment and longer builds.
- **Local community/government resistance** — concerns over environment, traffic, light/noise nuisance and use of local water/energy can cause legal/political delays or block projects entirely.
- **Land scarcity** — large plots are costly, especially in urban/space-constrained regions.
- **Fast tech change** — firms may wait for newer storage, compute, cooling or security tech, delaying plans.
- **Cloud-exit strategies and hybrid** — some firms prepare to move back to their own datacenters, adding hybrid complexity and cost.
- **Rising disruptions** — bigger datacenters serving more users need extra redundancy and backup.
- **Standardisation and multi-cloud management** — combining providers needs specialised knowledge and standardised tooling; Bas notes "Hier wordt vaak veel te makkelijk over gedacht" (people far too easily underestimate this).
