# Agile and PDCA as the unwritten cloud standard

Bas argues that for the implementation and adoption of cloud-based products, services and technologies, agile is more or less the (unwritten) standard. It is not so much a goal as a consequence you have to reckon with - a cultural shift that deserves attention.

Agile means short sprints delivering MVPs (he writes it out as both Minimum Valuable Product and Minimum Viable Product) that are continuously subject to change; a product can be an application or system, but also the implementation of a process. Working in iterations lets you learn fast from successes and mistakes and pivot quickly, though planning is sometimes underestimated and can eat a lot of time. His advice: often it is better to just start, however small, learn from it, then improve. The Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) method fits well because it drives continuous improvement.

He contrasts company sizes: larger organisations need solid preparation (e.g. assembling a separate team), whereas in smaller/SME firms it tends to happen more organically, or they are already used to this way of working - sometimes unconsciously.
