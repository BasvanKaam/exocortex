# RECs and the reality of cloud datacenter 'green' energy claims

Bas explains Renewable Energy Credits (RECs): tradable, non-tangible US energy commodities representing proof that 1 megawatt-hour of electricity was generated from an eligible renewable source and fed into the shared grid. A REC can be used only once and does not guarantee renewable energy is actually being consumed; it is essentially a way to fund (badly needed) renewable energy projects.

His caveat (2020 context): big providers' claims of running on 40-100% renewable energy aren't entirely true. They optimize servers and racks as efficiently as humanly possible, but they remain connected to main grids where fossil fuels (coal still no.1) generate electricity, and the green share actually consumed is often negligible. He also noted Dutch cloud datacenters being blocked from expansion due to local power shortages, with some near Amsterdam already drawing on emergency/back-up power.
