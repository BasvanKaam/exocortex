# Ten characteristics and benefits of a good IT roadmap

Bas's framing: in a good roadmap, technology choices go hand in hand with organization-driven changes; the two sides of a successful IT organization are interconnected, and this matters even more as solutions become cloud-based, because change has to be anchored within the IT team.

The ten:

1. **Overview, clarity and peace of mind (gemoedsrust).** A shared goal (the "stip op de horizon") for short, medium or long term; agreements on what, when, by whom, and which deliverables are expected when.
2. **An important part of your broader IT strategy.** The bridge between vision/wishes/requirements and actual execution; connects "the business" with technology.
3. **Well-substantiated decisions.** Preferably everything defined SMART.
4. **Reveals weak links** in the organization: organizational, technical, process, or a combination, so action can be taken.
5. **Helps set realistic goals**, e.g. overall timing (how long from A to B?) or a product/app implementation; evaluate and adjust in time to stay "op koers".
6. **Gives financial insight** and helps prioritize, based on "the demand" from the business (what is deemed critical), so time, money and people are deployed efficiently.
7. **Streamlines between business units.** Combining roadmaps of multiple BUs surfaces overlaps, enabling more efficient use of resources and lower development costs.
8. **A good story.** Lets you explain "the plan" simply to staff, clients, partners, suppliers and management; visualizing it often helps.
9. **Short communication lines** with shared responsibility and ownership, it is teamwork.
10. **Dynamic, not static.** Lets the team respond to events needing a change in approach, product or even the broader vision; a risk analysis is often part of it, and you can predefine when roadmap steps should be revisited.

Rule of thumb: roadmaps generally start from about three months out. Begin small if needed, keep a finger on the pulse, adjust in between.
