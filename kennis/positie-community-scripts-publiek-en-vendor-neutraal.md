# Community automation should be public and vendor-neutral

My stance, baked into how I ran the Scripted Actions Hackathon: the output of a community effort should be made publicly available, free for everyone, with or without the sponsoring vendor's product installed. Scripts should not have to be tied to Nerdio; they should run on native Azure too.

The reasoning: when the work is genuinely useful to the whole WVD/Azure community rather than locked to one product, everyone benefits, and the goodwill that generates does more for the vendor than gating the scripts ever would. Authors keep ownership of what they write; the vendor only adopts a script into its product library with explicit permission. Openness and contributor respect are the point, not an afterthought.
