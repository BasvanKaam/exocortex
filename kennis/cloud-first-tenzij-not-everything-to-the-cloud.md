# Cloud-First, But Not Everything to the Cloud

Bas's stance, argued at length against what he saw as the prevailing herd opinion (2017) that everything must move to the cloud, "linksom of rechtsom al is het maar omdat iedereen dat lijkt te roepen."

His position:
- "Cloud first, tenzij" explicitly includes the "tenzij": cloud is not always the best answer, sometimes on-premises fits better, and that should be said out loud and carried into advice.
- Lifting any on-premises application to the cloud "zo maar", regardless of how good/fast/stable the link is, is a bad idea. Windows applications will still be around for 10-15 years, probably longer, so the problem persists.
- A virtual desktop served from your own datacenter (when possible) almost always works better, faster and more reliably than its cloud counterpart. Some workloads you simply don't want to move.
- Many customers value keeping control of their own systems and data and don't want to depend on a third party. If you sense that at a customer, don't try to talk them out of it.
- Not every organization even wants cloud's "advantages"; for them it's a nice-to-have, so offer the best of both worlds.
- The answer is simply hybrid: e.g. the virtual desktop on-premises, plus SaaS, plus some infra (or DR) from the cloud. The combinations are endless.

The meta-argument: advise what best fits the customer, which can't possibly always be cloud-only. Daring to go against the established order is itself the differentiator. "Standaard alles naar de cloud, daar geloof ik niet in."
