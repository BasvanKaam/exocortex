# Bas's comfort-zone war story: the presentation training

Around 2010 Bas had collected nearly every certification imaginable in his field and felt stuck. He was convinced he would get no further than senior administrator or architect unless he learned to present — to stand in front of a group of IT'ers (who, when you start, you are convinced know it all better than you) and share knowledge, a vision, an opinion. This was the period he met Remco Nijkamp (author of *Vitaliteit*) as a colleague, and had a sort-of competence manager named Wim.

He gathered his courage and signed up for a two-day presentation training. He calls it the most terrible and confronting training he ever underwent — and precisely for that reason positive. The exercises went to the ridiculous: talking to each other in silly voices, standing in front of the group constantly. It was the furthest he had ever been from his comfort zone. Then came the harder part: actually applying it in practice, where Remco helped him enormously.

Bas's moral: do something you are not comfortable with, take on the challenge, see it as a contest with yourself where there can be only one winner. Don't be afraid what others think of you, make as many mistakes as possible, and enjoy it — because secretly it's all great fun. You won't regret it. It is one of the most important steps he ever took and (among other things) brought him where he is today.
