# Handwriting aids retention and idea flow (the RAS filter)

Durable concept Bas leans on: physically writing things down (actual pen and paper) helps absorb new information and make it stick better than typing. The brain has to work harder and is more 'involved', which he equates to being both efficient and effective.

His cited mechanism: writing stimulates the reticular activating system (RAS) at the base of the brain, which acts as a filter for everything the brain processes, giving more importance to what you are actively focusing on - something the physical act of writing brings to the forefront. Studies he references showed children in grades two, four and six wrote more words, faster, and expressed more ideas writing essays by hand versus with a keyboard.

*Bron: blogpost 'My version of why the pen is mightier than the keyboard' (2015-02-09), basvankaam.com.*
