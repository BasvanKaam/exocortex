# Shadow IT: The Business User in the Driver's Seat

Bas explains how easy cloud purchasing creates Shadow IT. Subscriptions are available everywhere, the threshold is low, and the business user now sits in the driver's seat — if someone isn't happy with internally offered services, staff go find their own solution. The era of the 'ivory tower', where only IT made tech decisions, is over.

**Shadow IT** = technology solutions bought or implemented by staff/departments without IT's knowledge or approval. Buyers get tools fast but bypass IT governance, creating security and operational risk. They're often unaware of the broader consequences: responsibility for maintenance, payments, policy compliance, updates/security patches, and integration with existing architecture.

**The double-edged sword (gemak versus duplicatie)** — self-choosing services gives flexibility, self-service, and speed, but the downside is IT's lack of control and overview. No department knows the processes, admin work, and risks as well as internal IT. Companies can end up paying double for services they didn't know IT already offered (Bas notes that's a fault on IT's side too). Bas's complementary point: IT must do far better at internally promoting existing solutions — if people don't know what's available and why, they'll go searching themselves.

Key quote: 'Selfservice IT gaat over verantwoordelijkheid.'
