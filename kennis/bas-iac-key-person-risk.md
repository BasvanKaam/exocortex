# Bas: IaC Creates Dangerous Key-Person Dependency

A second Bas practice example: an internal IT team of about five people manages a few thousand users and the associated Azure infrastructure, using IaC to keep it all running, patch, spin up new systems, auto-start/stop machines, etc. Two of the five are highly driven and skilled at IaC. Bas's warning: what happens when one of those two leaves (which does happen in this field), or when one or both is sick, on long holiday, etc.? You create a dangerous dependency if you're not careful, and it starts with being aware of the situation.

Bas also has a strong, characteristic opinion on *who* can do IaC: just pointing at someone on your team to "go learn IaC" doesn't work. People who are good at and driven by IaC are usually not your average IT'er; they spend a lot of time on self-education, love difficult situations, and are often working late evenings or weekends finding solutions, writing blogs, preparing presentations. IaC is not for everyone; you need a certain passion for it.
