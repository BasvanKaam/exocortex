# Bas: The 6 R's for Application Rationalization

Bas frames applications as by far (always) the biggest stumbling block in Cloud migrations, or any migration. You can leave everything alone ("out of scope"), saving time and money, but as he puts it: **"als je doet wat je altijd al deed, krijg je wat je altijd al kreeg"** (if you do what you always did, you get what you always got). Rationalizing your application landscape adds work but yields big benefits, e.g. discovering apps barely used yet kept alive on substantial infrastructure, where the R of **Retire** is the answer.

The 6 R's as Bas defines them:
- **Rehost**: traditional lift and shift. Whether it's wise depends on the situation; you learn the Cloud fast and workloads are easier to optimize once already in the Cloud.
- **Replatform**: small optimizations without changing the application source code, e.g. using an underlying PaaS platform (databases, web servers, Windows Virtual Desktop).
- **Repurchasing**: buy a different equivalent product, usually a Cloud-native SaaS alternative.
- **Refactor / re-architect**: the application is rewritten at source-code level, rebuilt on Cloud-native technology, to improve performance or add features; for business-critical workloads. The most expensive variant.
- **Retire**: organizations quickly have hundreds of apps, many barely used; a precise inventory reveals which to retire.
- **Retain**: keep what you have for now; no direct reason to move (can't/may not, just invested, or it simply feels/performs better on-premises).

After the inventory, use the SCAF Wave 1&2 templates to write a brief summary of findings.

(Note: in Opdracht 7 Bas lists a slightly different 6 R variant: Rehost, Refactor, Rearchitect, Rebuild, Replace, Retire.)
