# Lessons Learned: Running a Community Hackathon

Bas's own retrospective after the first Nerdio Scripted Actions Hackathon. Reusable operational lessons for running a vendor community event:

**Lead time.** Two months in advance is cutting it close; plan three or more.

**Legal first, not last.** The NDA was the biggest hiccup: getting it drafted, sent, signed. Have the e-signature tooling (e.g. HelloSign) set up beforehand so you know what it can and cannot do, and send NDAs as early as possible. Have a documented process; most of this is reusable next time.

**Panel early.** Assemble the review panel at the very start, before you even start promoting; it takes time.

**Central submission repository.** You need one place to collect scripts that every panelist can access. A plain shared inbox does not work.

**Separate event ops from marketing tooling.** Running everything through the marketing platform (HubSpot) means every message looks like a campaign email and open rates drop, which hurts exactly when you need NDAs signed and the kickoff sent. You need the ability to email contestants outside the marketing system, and the permissions/ability to export contact lists yourself rather than depending on one gatekeeper.

**Signup hygiene.** Send a confirmation email/message on signup (people asked when they got none). Stop including people who already signed up in the "please sign up" campaign. Set a hard cutoff date after which signup closes, so downstream steps (NDAs, kickoff) stay in sync instead of new people trickling in.

**Ownership.** Tasks were spread too thinly across functions and departments. Better to have one or two people own the whole thing (or the biggest part) and pull in help as needed.
