# Building a Knowledge Organization (Detron Playbook)

A concrete, repeatable playbook Bas used at Detron (as Technology Officer) to turn an IT integrator into a recognized knowledge organization over roughly three years. The throughline: knowledge sharing IS marketing. "Betere reclame kun je, je als bedrijf niet wensen."

The moves:
- A recurring informal trigger event (the "Detron Bloggers Borrel") grew active bloggers from 2-3 to 12 company-wide. A low-friction social ritual outperformed top-down mandates for getting people to publish.
- Encourage colleagues to attend AND present at conferences, domestically and abroad. Visibility for the professional pulls the company name along with it.
- Selflessly sharing knowledge in the community produces recognition that compounds: community-leader status plus vendor awards (Citrix CTP/CTA, VMware vExpert, Nutanix NTC, RES RSVP). From zero awarded colleagues in mid-2014 to five.
- Internal knowledge sharing run by domain-aligned business developers (Connectivity, Security, Cloud/On-premises, Workspace), each owning sessions in their domain. Format ranges from 50-75 person technical sessions to weekend soldering sessions still drawing 20+ colleagues.
- Regular informal brainstorm sessions (pizza, a beer, group thinking on how to respond to new tech developments). Security cited as a recent example.
- Process certifications alongside tech: Prince2, ITIL V3, Scrum Foundation, producing dozens of certified staff.
- A Technology Board acting as sounding board, feeding an internal knowledge cafe (the "DiK" - Detron ICT Kennis Cafe).

The insight worth keeping: a knowledge organization is built bottom-up through rituals, recognition, and giving people room to share, not declared top-down.
