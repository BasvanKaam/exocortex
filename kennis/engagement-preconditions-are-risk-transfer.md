# Engagement Preconditions Are a Risk-Transfer Contract

Bas treats the preconditions (randvoorwaarden) section of a proposal as a working clause, not boilerplate. The stance: an advice is only as good as the facts it rests on, so before committing to a deliverable and a deadline he names exactly what the client must supply, and ties the consequences of not supplying it back to the client.

The preconditions he sets are concrete and access-oriented: a representative group of stakeholders made available, relevant IT partners reachable where applicable, IT management available, insight into existing infrastructure documentation, the governing policy documents (information-security policy, IT policy, IT strategy, GDPR/AVG documentation), and actual access to the current infrastructure.

The load-bearing sentence is the one that follows the list: these conditions exist so the advice can be guaranteed to rest on the right facts and be delivered within the agreed time, and if they are not met there is a real risk of delay, inaccuracy, and additional cost. That single line converts "help us out here" into a shared-responsibility contract. If inputs are withheld, the timeline and quality risk move to the client, by prior agreement, not to the consultant.

This pairs with his insistence on full commitment from board to end-user level: he wants a complete picture so every facet is represented and the client can make the right choices. Preconditions are how that commitment is made concrete and enforceable.
