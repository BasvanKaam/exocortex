# Hyperconverged Infrastructure: Three Use Cases

Bas frames hyperconverged (HC) infrastructure as a high-value building block in a hybrid-cloud proposition, with three distinct applications (originally articulated in the Detron/Nutanix context but conceptually durable):

1. **On-premises replacement** (fully or partly). For organizations thinking about cloud but not ready to jump, for whatever reason. Investing in legacy three-tier environments is no longer the smart choice. HC gives you the "cloud economics" benefits without becoming dependent on a cloud provider: data stays in your own management, everything stays close to the user, and integration with SaaS or other cloud services remains easy.
2. **Private (hybrid) cloud**: partly on-premises, partly in the (provider) cloud. A way to get acquainted with cloud benefits (flexibility, scalability, time-to-market) and slowly expand cloud usage, while most infrastructure stays on-premises with limited SaaS. Makes a future move much easier.
3. **Hybrid cloud**: infrastructure split between on-premises and cloud (IaaS/PaaS), augmented with SaaS. Build out step by step. What you want or must keep in-house stays on-premises, partly because many on-premises apps are not (yet) available as a SaaS alternative.

The through-line: HC as a measured, low-dependency path along the on-premises-to-cloud spectrum.
