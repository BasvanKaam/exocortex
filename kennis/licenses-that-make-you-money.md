# A license is free if it returns more than it costs

Bas's commercial argument: a Nerdio license shouldn't be evaluated as a cost line but as an investment that returns more than it consumes. When you factor in the Azure-resource savings, the time saved on repetitive tasks, and the added efficiency, the license doesn't cost money, it makes money. His reframing is that the licenses are effectively free, because the savings they unlock exceed the license fee (research cited puts payback at an average of ~6.5 days and savings up to ~83% of Azure resource cost). The honesty/flexibility of the model, consumption-based, in arrears, no lock-in, is part of the same pitch: a fair model that aligns vendor and customer. For the commercially-minded buyer he keeps it about the result, with the technical depth available on request.
