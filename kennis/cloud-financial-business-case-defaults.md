# The defaults-exposing cloud financial business case

Bas teaches cloud economics with a worked comparison across four delivery options: on-prem, co-location, managed services and cloud. The teaching trick: cloud comes out slightly cheaper, but only because of a long stack of assumed defaults, which he then lists to show how hard the comparison really is. Example defaults that swing the number:

- A 3-year reserved capacity assumption (saves 40%+).
- Standard HDDs, not SSDs.
- Linux (no license cost) instead of Windows.
- Prices per region; not all regions offer the same options/products; price differs per option/product per region.
- VM types, and more.

He pairs this with the point that a business case is far more than money alone (worked out in a table), and uses a Gartner multi-year overview to explain real ROI. Closing message: the benefits of cloud are not always directly financially visible; they often hide in hard-to-measure variables - the same 'cloud promises' resurfacing here.
