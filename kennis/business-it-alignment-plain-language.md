# Business-IT Alignment In Plain Language

Bas's framing of a phrase that gets thrown around without definition. IT solutions, usually technical in nature, exist to help achieve business goals; the wants, requirements and objectives of 'the business' should steer the broader strategic IT plan. He calls this an old-fashioned idea that is no less relevant for being old.

**Who 'the business' is:** the people who set the strategic company objectives. Varies per organization, but typically the CEO, COO and CFO, often joined by the CTO, sales, HR, marketing leads and department managers.

**Why the gap forms:** little or no joint work, so neither side knows what the other is doing or expects. No structural meeting cadence, jargon creates confusion, knowledge is not shared.

Typical complaints he lists:
- About the business: decisions made without involving IT; objectives not SMART; constant changes of mind; shadow IT (external resources used without IT's knowledge).
- About IT: takes too long; too expensive or unclear where the money goes; they don't understand what's needed; they say no a lot; no clear story.

**Where the magic happens (his fix):** collaborate more closely, involve each other, listen well and ask questions. Make clear agreements and record expectations (define what success looks like) to avoid misunderstandings. Meet regularly and, where possible, create an internal exchange program. Since many organizations work Agile well beyond software now, invite a board member to a daily standup or, better, an upcoming sprint planning. Afterwards, over coffee, it quickly becomes clear whether everyone is still pointed the same way.

The payoff: IT changes step by step into a strategic, innovative partner, enabling competitive advantages, new markets, and faster line-of-sight to the company objectives.
