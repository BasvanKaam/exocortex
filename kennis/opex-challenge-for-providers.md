# Opex Is Harder for the Provider Than the Customer

A war-story reframe from Bas's MSP work: for cloud providers the Opex story is different and challenging, because constant income is hard to predict — they must weigh infrastructure costs against fluctuating customer usage. That's why many providers impose minimum take/commitment periods to stabilise revenue, something Bas says he encounters almost daily at the various Managed Service Providers he works with. It's a different way of thinking, doing, and ultimately billing; some creativity is required, and getting insight into your monthly spend is crucial. Customers generally prefer to pay a pre-agreed price.

Bas has helped many an MSP assemble 'packages' at a fixed price per workplace per month. It sounds simple but in practice often isn't. The classic tiers are **bronze, silver, gold, platinum** ("Brons, zilver, goud, platina"), where each package offers different VM types (more/less compute and storage), a portion of software maintenance and patching, services like first- and second-line helpdesk, and a standard layer of applications depending on the chosen tier.

Bottom line: for the supplier, Opex means income is uncertain; a balance between costs and revenue must be found. As a result, Opex-based services end up somewhat more expensive — especially under a pure pay-as-you-go model.
