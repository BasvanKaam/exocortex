# Call them traditional Windows apps, not legacy, and AVD keeps them alive

His stance: there is a misconception that web and SaaS apps have taken over. In reality millions of native Windows applications are still in use by thousands of companies worldwide, some firms running over a thousand apps, and new Windows apps are released and updated daily. He argues for calling these 'traditional' rather than 'legacy.' Transforming or replacing a single application with a SaaS/web alternative can take weeks to months and raises the question of what happens to the associated data; at scale this is a multi-year effort. AVD's crucial role is keeping these traditional applications alive and usable so companies keep serving users while modernizing where possible, because modernization cannot happen overnight. This reframes AVD's value: not a stopgap but the platform that sustains the real-world Windows application landscape.
