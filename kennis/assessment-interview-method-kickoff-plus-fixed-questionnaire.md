# Assessment interview method: kick-off plus a fixed end-user questionnaire

My intake for a workplace/infrastructure engagement runs as a structured interview round, and the structure is deliberate.

## Two interview tracks
- **One kick-off interview** with the operational/business owner (e.g. the operations manager). This one I write out in full in the report because it sets the organizational and technical frame: how a normal workday looks, what management work happens, what projects are running that could make or break things, what the biggest challenge is, the stance on BYOD, licensing policy, and so on. From this interview I also ask the owner to **name the key users** I should sit down with next — the snowball that builds the rest of the round.
- **A spread of end-user / role interviews** (managers, admin staff, regional managers, IT). For these I summarize only the most striking findings, "if applicable". The IT staff interviews are used differently: not the questionnaire, but to map the actual backend, technologies, products, and how they are experienced in practice.

## The fixed questionnaire (end users)
Pre-defined questions, tuned to the agreed assignment, supplemented by whatever comes up live ("not everything can be planned"). The set I use covers experience, not just facts:
1. What type of workplace do you have (mobile, fixed, other)?
2. Are you satisfied with the mobility offered?
3. How do you experience sharing information with colleagues?
4. Are you satisfied with working-from-home options?
5. During the day, what are your most-used applications?
6. Do disruptions occur? Can you give an example?
7. How do you experience IT support in general?
8. How do you experience the current way of working?
9. Are you missing any functionality?
10. What could or should be different, in your view?
11. How do you experience [the new tool] so far?
12. **What grade (1–10) would you give your workplace / way of working?**
13. Anything else you want to add?

## Why the grade question matters
Question 12 turns soft sentiment into a number I can report: "lowest grade a 7, highest a 9, average an 8." That single line tells the client the baseline satisfaction before any change, and it doubles as a before-measurement for after the migration. Cheap to ask, disproportionately useful.

## Principles
- Interviews are the **primary information source**; everything else (existing docs, health checks, app lists) is combined on top.
- The kick-off interview earns its full write-up because it is the project frame; the rest earn summaries.
- Talk to the **end user about experience**, talk to **IT about the plumbing** — different questions for different roles.
