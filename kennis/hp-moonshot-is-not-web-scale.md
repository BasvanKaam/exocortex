# HP Moonshot is converged but not truly web-scale

Bas drew a sharp definitional line (June 2014). Reacting to the Nutanix-Dell deal, he argued Dell/Nutanix would outclass HP's Moonshot because Moonshot 'is still much more about the hardware involved.' He questioned whether 'software defined' or 'web-scale' even apply to Moonshot: 'I'm thinking no, should you say converged than yes.'

He engaged HP's own claim that skipping a hypervisor reduces complexity and improves security, but countered with the capabilities he sees as web-scale's essence: 'what about flexibility, automatic failovers, data tiering, data dedup, replicated writes and so on?' His verdict is fair rather than dismissive: both can serve VDI and big-data workloads, they just go about it differently, and Moonshot has real advantages, 'it's just not Web-Scale material, not yet anyway.' Shows him defining a category precisely and policing the marketing language around it.
