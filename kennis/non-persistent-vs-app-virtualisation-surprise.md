# If most VDIs are non-persistent, why aren't more apps virtualised?

Observation/framing (March 2015): looking at the VRC 2015 numbers, Bas finds it 'somewhat surprising' that with the majority of VDIs being non-persistent (stateless), the share of virtualised or layered applications wasn't higher. His reasoning: a stateless desktop logically pushes you toward delivering apps separately (virtualised/layered) rather than baking them in, so he expected the layering number to track the non-persistent number more closely.

He doesn't resolve it dogmatically - he floats hypotheses (apps that can't be virtualised, wrong tech, lack of knowledge, SBC-installed apps skewing the data) and explicitly declines to relitigate the layering debate: 'a lot of pros and cons surrounding this specific subject making it a (potentially) hard one to get your head around, which I'm not going to try at this time.' Captures his data-curious, non-dogmatic stance on the persistent/non-persistent debate.
