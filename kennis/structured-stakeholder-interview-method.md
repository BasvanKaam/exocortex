# Bas's structured stakeholder interview method

To build a clear picture of the current situation, pain points, specific requirements and wishes, and organizational goals, Bas gathers information through a round of individual interviews before writing anything. He interviews across the management/leadership layer and adds a separate, earlier in-depth conversation with the IT department, so both the business and technical sides are heard.

How he runs it:

- **A small, fixed question set, tuned to the agreed assignment.** He works from predefined questions but treats them as a frame, not a script: he explicitly leaves room for the interviewee's own input and for whatever else comes up, because "not everything can be planned."
- **He records who was interviewed** - name, role, date - in a table in the report, so the basis of his findings is traceable.

His recurring question arc (generalized):

1. Introduce yourself and your role in the organization.
2. This change has been considered for a while - what role did you play in it?
3. What are the main reasons (wishes and requirements) for wanting it?
4. How do you experience the current way of working / sharing information today? Which tools are used?
5. What are the drawbacks, what do you miss as an organization, and what is indispensable in the future? Do you think this change solves it?
6. This is a big job with technical, financial and organizational dependencies and a long lead time - do you think everyone is sufficiently aware of that?
7. How would you describe the organization's capacity to adopt change?
8. Anything else you want to raise?

Notice the shape: he opens with role and involvement, moves to drivers and current pain, then deliberately tests two things many sponsors underestimate - awareness of effort/lead time, and the organization's adoption appetite. He closes with an open question. After the round he writes a short synthesis of where people agree and disagree, and feeds that straight into the conclusions.

*Bron: geanonimiseerde aanpak uit een Salomon-IT klantopdracht (a shared-service organization for municipal real estate and sports facilities (joint venture with municipalities)).*
