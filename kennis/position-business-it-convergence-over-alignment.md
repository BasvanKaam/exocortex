# Position: pursue business-IT convergence, not alignment

In the SCAF Strategy template's points-of-attention block, Bas takes an explicit stance against the common 'business-IT alignment' framing. His argument:

- 'Alignment' implies you still treat business and IT as two separate camps/departments. 'Convergence' fits better: not aligned-but-separate, but the two becoming one. More focus should go here.
- The usual reality is a 'gap': the two don't collaborate, don't understand each other, aren't on the same line, don't know what the other does or needs, use their own jargon, don't share knowledge, and make business decisions without involving IT (or without making them SMART).
- He names both sides' grievances: business says IT takes too long, is too expensive, says no too often, 'just does stuff'; IT says the business keeps changing its mind, doesn't explain what it does, decides things without asking, and procures external IT around them.
- His prescription: IT must be, or become, a strategic, innovative and equal partner to the business, each complementing the other, both working toward the same goal. IT solutions must serve business needs and goals - business objectives should drive (or contribute to) the strategic IT plan.
