# Start small, test, then scale — because it de-risks the client's investment

My default position on any build/migration I advise: **start small, test thoroughly, and only then take the next step.** The reason I give the client is explicit and financial — this is how you run *minimal risk on the required investment*.

## What it looks like in practice
- Begin with a tiny footprint: a couple of virtual desktop hosts, one or two of the client/server apps rebuilt in the cloud, a small slice of user data and profiles, no backup yet. Prove it, then expand.
- Run the **test phase on pay-as-you-go** so machines can be switched off and resized freely; move to reserved instances only once the shape is proven and quantities are known.
- Decide and record **which workloads/apps/data migrate first, and why** — selection is a deliberate, justified choice, not whatever is easiest.
- Accept a **temporary period of two parallel environments** while users move over step by step, with support and training arranged in consultation.
- Make **up-front agreements** about planning, expected outcome, support (including on-site), and who owns which responsibility, *before* the build starts.

## The 0.9 → 1.0 close
I present the report as a **0.9 version** first: the key technological choices are explained functionally, with full room for questions and feedback. Only after that feedback is incorporated do I deliver a **1.0** and re-share it with everyone involved. The advice itself is never the final word — it's a draft that earns its 1.0 by surviving the client's questions.

## Why I hold this position
A big-bang cutover bets the whole investment on assumptions that the intake could not fully verify. A staged approach lets reality correct the plan cheaply: "the build continues, or the plans are adjusted where needed based on the first tests." It keeps the client's exposure proportional to what has actually been proven to work.
