# The XenDesktop App Edition licensing fiasco and XenApp's comeback

Bas tells the licensing war story around XenDesktop 7.0. With XenApp gone, Citrix introduced 'XenDesktop App Edition' (alongside VDI, Enterprise and Platinum) to replace the old XenApp 6.5 licenses and deliver XenApp features.

What went wrong: Citrix announced that XenApp Enterprise and Platinum customers with active Subscription Advantage could move to the App edition at no charge. What that really meant: customers on XenApp Advanced edition (even with active Subscription Advantage) were out of luck and had to buy new XenDesktop Enterprise/Platinum licenses. Otherwise they were stuck on the IMA, unable to use Windows Server 2012 R2. Bas calls this 'a bad marketing move to say the least', noting customers couldn't care less about the underlying architecture, they just want a cost-effective, robust solution that works.

The comeback: Citrix listened. They established a separate license Trade-Up Program for XenApp 6.5 Advanced edition customers, and on 26 March 2014 reinstated XenApp and XenDesktop 7.5 as separate products and downloads, with Web Interface support. Bas notes this was 'more marketing than anything else: technically nothing changed', you download the same bits and, depending on license, install XenApp or XenDesktop. At Citrix Synergy May 2015 they went further, announcing extended support for XenApp 6.5 (end of maintenance moved from 24 Feb 2016 to 31 Dec 2017; end of life from 24 Aug 2016 to 30 Jun 2018) for customers deploying Feature Pack 3 for XenApp 6.5. 'A great applause followed.'
