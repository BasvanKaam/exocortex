# SCAF Design Principles and Why It Exists

Bas designed SCAF because companies need a proven, structured approach to introduce, implement/adopt and maintain new cloud technologies and models. The choice in cloud is enormous (one of cloud's pitfalls) and people quickly lose sight of where to start. SCAF provides direction, clarity and steering — before, during and after a cloud migration or implementation. Its principles:

- **Insight and choices:** the framework maps the relevant factors in detail so you can make the right choices, highlighting all pros and any cons per case.
- **Cloud vendor-neutral:** you are in no way bound to Microsoft, Google, AWS or others; you pick the platform that best fits the use case.
- **Hand in hand:** business objectives, requirements and wishes are inventoried; technology choices go hand in hand with organisational changes. A possible Cloud Center of Excellence (CCoE) comes into play here. Cloud adoption success begins long before a vendor is chosen.
- **Cultural changes:** introducing cloud can cause cultural shifts — not a goal in itself, but often a consequence; an agile/DevOps approach (Plan, Do, Check, Act) may be introduced.
- **Modernising through iterations:** although lift-and-shift is the obvious route, investing time to modernise existing applications/data/systems can pay off more than it costs; done across iterations within the execution phase, letting the organisation learn early.
- **Adoption, Governance, Beheer:** adoption must be embraced and promoted by leadership and users; governance goes beyond tech (responsibilities, policy, compliance, risk, portfolio, licensing, cost control); chosen solutions must be managed (release/change management, monitoring, patching, security).
- **Skills-readiness plan:** gaps in knowledge are identified across SCAF phases; a skills-readiness plan is a deliverable.
- **Full lifecycle:** SCAF covers the complete lifecycle, expanding the cloud-service portfolio step by step and revising/replacing/removing solutions over time.

Bas illustrates the step-by-step approach with his elephant joke.
