# XenApp 7.5 is a name change dressed as a comeback, and that's fine

Reacting in real time (late January 2014) to the XenApp 7.5 announcement, Bas's stance is openly skeptical-but-pragmatic. He read the "back by popular demand" framing as mostly marketing: "with regards to FMA, it's more of a marketing campaign than anything else." He notes the community reaction skewed sceptical, annoyed, amazed and sad, with "happy" conspicuously missing, and that people were "still in shock."

He consistently models a measured posture, telling readers to "hold your horses": yes it changes again, but no, you don't start over, because it's still FMA. He works to find the upside ("I'm trying to be positive here!", "the glass is half full!") and lands on caring about capability over naming: "I couldn't care less about the name (although, marketing wise, I realize it's very important) just as long as they keep rocking those bits and bytes."

His open questions centre on licensing fairness, whether existing XD 7.x customers with SA, and former XenApp Advanced customers pushed onto XenDesktop App Edition, can migrate without extra cost, showing his instinct to protect the customer's interest amid rebrands.
