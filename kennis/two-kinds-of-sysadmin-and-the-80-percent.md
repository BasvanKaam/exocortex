# The two kinds of sysadmin, and why 80% of IT should not have to go all-in

Bas's recurring stance about IT people and tooling. He splits IT pros into two kinds:

1. The tech-savvy 'I really like to dig in' sysadmin who deep-dives whenever they can, blogs, helps on forums, contributes to community programs. For them work becomes hobby (or vice versa). Not for everyone, far from it, and that's OK.
2. The all-round, laid-back (in a good way) sysadmin who does not want to spend free time getting to know new tech, likes 'to the point' graphical interfaces, keeps certifications current but will not become a PowerShell/scripting/automation guru. 'It's a job they love, it gives them fulfillment, but that's it. And that is more than OK.'

His estimate: this second group is about 80% of people in IT, give or take.

The argument that follows: automation is great and he encourages technically-oriented folks to dig in and learn, but you must be realistic from a business perspective. Real hands-on experience takes a lot of time, DevOps/automation can be tricky to maintain, and it is not for everybody. Therefore tooling that lets the 80% build, manage, price and optimize WVD/Azure is the realistic answer, not expecting everyone to become an automation expert.

He is deliberately non-judgmental toward both groups ('And that's OK, too'). This is a values position, not just a sales line: he respects the all-rounder rather than treating depth as the only legitimate path.
