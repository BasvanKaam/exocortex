# Idea: Citrix Reskill course, welcome video, mid-course support offer, Reskill Edition and a new Innovation Day

Bas's proposal to Joseph Landis (Nerdio president) around the Citrix Reskill course: 16 modules that map what an on-premises Citrix admin knows to AVD and Windows 365, with Nerdio on top, plus a free Nerdio install for the hands-on work (working name: Reskill Edition). The guiding principle: help first, and any sales conversation comes late and only for people who have shown they are serious, which is why none of the offers below are mentioned at the start or in promotion.

Open points: where the support offer sits (module 6, 7 or 8), the user threshold (1,000 or 1,500), the user cap and length of the free production use, and which module people continue with after the Innovation Day afternoon (4 or 5).

## Draft message (BvK voice, English)

**Subject:** Citrix Reskill course, a few ideas I'd like your take on

Hi Joseph,

I'm deep into the Citrix Reskill course at the moment, and while building it a few ideas came up that go a bit beyond the course itself, so before I take any of them further I'd like to hear what you think.

A quick recap of where we are. The course takes everything a Citrix admin has built up over 5, 10 or 15 years of running things on-premises and maps it, in a lot of detail and across 16 modules, to Azure Virtual Desktop and Windows 365, with Nerdio on top (a Citrix site becomes a workspace, and so on). Next to that there's a free Nerdio install for the hands-on work, which I'd like to call the Reskill Edition.

**One principle first, because everything below depends on it**

None of this should feel like marketing. The course is there to help Citrix admins move forward, and that's how it needs to come across from the first lesson to the last. Where it does turn into a commercial conversation, that happens later on, and only with people who have shown they're serious. That's also why we don't mention any of the offers below at the start, in the course description or when we promote it, because then the whole thing turns into a sales funnel with some lessons attached, and Citrix admins, who have been sold to plenty over the years, pick up on that right away.

**1. A short welcome from you**

Before people get to Welcome lesson 1 with the full agenda, a video of two to three minutes where you welcome them, tell them how glad we are they're here and get them a bit excited about what's coming. No longer than that.

**2. Technical support, offered halfway through the course**

Somewhere around module 6, 7 or 8 (we still need to decide exactly where), we offer a set number of hours per week of technical support to help them build out, migrate and test. We don't mention it in any other module, before or after. People can open the modules in any order, because they think they know the rest already, or because they do, so the offer stays in that one module and doesn't follow them around.

The thinking behind it is that someone who has worked their way that far into the course has skin in the game, they're putting real effort in, and if they reach out to us at that point, chances are they're a hot lead already. Existing Nerdio customers, or people who found us another way because they were looking to move off Citrix anyway, will think "we're getting help already", and that's fine. For new customers who follow the lessons in order, I think this could work very well.

A few conditions, without asking anyone to sign anything or commit to anything:

- The migration covers at least 1,000 to 1,500 users moving off Citrix (exact number to agree).
- They've done the hands-on steps in the earlier modules. Some will click straight to this module and want to use it right away, which is fine, but then we ask them to go back and do the hands-on first, unless they've already covered it some other way. Often the steps that didn't work are the reason they want help in the first place, so that's useful for us to know too.

Some will drop out along the way, run out of time or decide not to go ahead, that always happens, but I don't expect that from most of them.

This needs sales, presales and our sales engineers on board, since it could get a bit busier for them and they'll need to reserve time for it. It isn't very different from how we work today though. In my experience 70 to 80% of the customers we talk to are looking for an alternative to the VDI platform they run now, Citrix, Omnissa or something else, so this gives those conversations a much better starting point.

**3. The Reskill Edition, also in production for a while**

Next to the course, the Reskill Edition of Nerdio Manager for Enterprise, and maybe three months free in a production environment as well, if needed with a cap on the number of users so we can keep an eye on it. The more of those steps they take with it, the closer we get to a win.

**4. A new take on Innovation Days**

In the morning we cover the theory and the why, much like we've done before, and we can fill that ourselves without Microsoft. A bit of EUC background too, without making fun of the companies in question of course, but these people don't come to a day like that for nothing. In the afternoon we do modules 1, 2 and 3, which is more or less an Innovation Day as we used to run it, and all familiar ground for them, because it's "this is what you always did in Citrix, and this is how we do it now in AVD, Windows 365 and Intune, with Nerdio." That tends to change how people look at it, once they see how easy it can be. After that they carry on for free with module 4 or 5 and the rest of the course, building on what we covered in the morning, with the Reskill Edition next to it.

**What I'd like from you**

First of all your gut reaction. Does this make sense to you, what would you change, and is there anything here you'd rather we didn't do? If you like the direction, I'll work out the details with sales and the SEs.

Thanks,
Bas
