# A company built on family, not a family business

Bas takes a stance on what good company culture actually looks like, grounded in a personal experience: when someone in his immediate circle had a health situation that needed his attention, Salomon's management immediately asked how *he* was doing and what they could do, and arranged calamity and care leave for him without him having to ask, as if it were the most normal thing in the world. Colleagues asked daily how things were going and offered to pick up work, for him and his family.

His argument: there is always workload and everyone wants to perform so the business grows, but at the end of the day you do that because you believe in the strength of the company and the people you get to work with. The distinction he draws is deliberate: this is not a 'family business' (familiebedrijf), but a business *based on* family, including the norms and values that come with it. He is proud of and grateful for that.

The underlying position: a culture that puts the person before the work in a crisis is what earns genuine belief and loyalty from its people. Time and space granted in a hard moment is repaid in conviction.
