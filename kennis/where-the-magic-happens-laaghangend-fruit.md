# Where The Magic Happens and Het Laaghangend Fruit

Bas's practical recipe ('Where The Magic Happens') for closing the IT-business gap: collaborate more closely, involve each other, listen well, ask questions. Make clear agreements on who holds which responsibility, communicate in understandable language, and record expectations to prevent misunderstandings. Consult regularly and, where possible, create an internal exchange programme. Setting and working toward a joint end goal greatly raises the chance of success. He suggests inviting a board member to a daily stand-up or sprint planning — afterwards, over coffee, it quickly becomes clear whether everyone is still aligned. This way IT step by step becomes a strategic, innovative partner.

**Het laaghangend fruit (low-hanging fruit)** — Bas's checklist: no jargon, use understandable language; consult regularly (fixed place, time, interval); clarify who carries which responsibility; record expectations and agreements; exchange and involve each other more; daily stand-up or sprint-planning meeting; set joint goals and have fun (book an outing, go bowling, eat well, get to know each other).

He deliberately calls it 'low-hanging fruit' because while reading you think 'of course, obvious' — yet in practice it rarely happens. Often success is just applying what we already know; we only need to be reminded, which is what he is doing.
