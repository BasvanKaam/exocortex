# Chapter 6 Key Takeaways: Capex/Opex, hosting models, business case, VDI/DaaS

Bas's summary of Chapter 6:
- Right IT-infrastructure choices begin with understanding the financial and operational models, with the Capex vs Opex choice central. Capex (capital investments) lets companies keep ownership and control via large one-off spends (systems, own datacenters) and put those assets on the balance sheet, adding long-term value. Opex (operational expenditure), as with cloud, shifts IT spend to ongoing costs - more flexibility, but depending on usage can be more expensive long term and gives less direct control.
- Hosting choice depends on organisational needs: on-premises gives maximum control but full responsibility for maintenance/management; co-location partly splits those responsibilities; Managed Services and Cloud take much of the work off your hands.
- A good business case looks beyond cost to include ROI, TCO and strategic impact, enabling well-considered decisions aligned to both financial and operational goals.
- VDI vs DaaS: VDI delivers a virtual desktop environment managed in your own datacenter; DaaS offers the same concept fully delivered from the cloud. DaaS's pay-as-you-go model lowers initial costs and enables fast scalability.
- Single-user vs multi-user adds another layer of options: single-user offers more customisation and isolation, multi-user is often cheaper and more efficient, especially for standard environments.

Bas's overarching point: the Capex/Opex choice and the choice between infrastructure/desktop models depend strongly on an organisation's needs and goals; a clear business case plus modern tech (DaaS, multi-user) lets companies optimise IT strategy and be ready for the future.
