# The Cloud Is Not Automatically Cheaper, And 'No Upfront Investment' Is Often False

Bas takes an explicit stance against two pieces of cloud marketing orthodoxy. First: the cloud is not cheaper by definition, 'het ligt er aan' (it depends). Second: the claim of 'no upfront investment' is, in his view, not always true. If you lift-and-shift your datacenter into the cloud, you get the same maintenance-heavy estate and no cost saving; if you do it right with Serverless/PaaS/IaC, you must invest time and money upfront before the long-term savings arrive. Either way the easy 'no investment' promise breaks. His positive position is consistent with his recurring theme: go for the best solution, always, even if that means a more traditional approach, and decide by mapping the pros and cons together with the client rather than chasing the cloud as a default. The deeper stance: the real obstacle to good cloud is mindset, people keep thinking in traditional IT terms and that, more than technology, slows adoption.
