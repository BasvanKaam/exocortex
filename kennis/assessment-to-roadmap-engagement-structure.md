# Four-Step Assessment-to-Roadmap Engagement Structure

The concrete execution recipe Bas runs when a client (here, an interim IT manager at a pharmaceutical company) asks for a route out of an aging, fragmented IT landscape toward a defined target. It turns the website-level Assessment-to-Roadmap proposition into four sequenced steps. The deliberate ordering matters: each step feeds the next, so information momentum is not lost.

**Step 1: Policy, mission, vision and strategy.** Talk to stakeholders about the IT policy in place, the organization's mission and vision, and how those align with the stated strategy (for example a "cloud-first, unless" / "Microsoft, unless" direction). Ask explicitly whether anything else is in play that the advice must account for: mergers, acquisitions, budget. Board-level commitment is flagged here as decisive, and budget is surfaced early so a compact business case can underpin the eventual recommendation if needed.

**Step 2: Inventory the IT landscape and applications.** Map the current environment in detail: workplace (physical and virtual), security, infrastructure, and how applications are invoked and behave on the workplace. The output is a detailed picture of the central and decentral environment and how end users and the admin team actually use it.

**Step 3: Capture functional requirements and wishes.** Building on steps 1 and 2, draw out requirements and wishes across applications, infrastructure, and workplace. Filter them on necessity (nut en noodzaak) as a first pass. Define, together with the client, a representative group to interview, covering at minimum: internal IT (infra/workplace beheer), internal IT (application beheer), and existing IT partners. The people chosen must be representative of their knowledge domain so the time invested is used optimally.

**Step 4: 0.9 report, present, then FINAL plus roadmap.** Steps 1 to 3 together establish the current state (IST), the desired state (SOLL), and the overarching strategy and policy. These are worked into a detailed report containing two clear, client-fitting options and a clear recommendation. The report is shared as a 0.9 version and presented in a separate session so the context of the findings lands and the client can ask questions, give feedback, and choose which option to develop further. Feedback is then folded into a FINAL advisory report, which is translated into a visual roadmap.

The IST / SOLL / strategy triangle is the analytical backbone, and the 0.9-then-FINAL cadence keeps the client deciding rather than the consultant deciding for them.
