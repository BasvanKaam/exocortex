# Idea: Bridge! - A Structured Internal Mentoring Program

Bas's own pitch (taken to the internal 'shark tank'). The premise: a fast-growing company hires faster than it onboards, so pair new hires with seasoned colleagues to accelerate their **Time To Target**, retain talent, and transfer knowledge in a structured way rather than ad hoc.

**Prime objectives:** employee retention/satisfaction, boosting productivity (Time To Target), more structured knowledge transfer, engagement, building relationships, and having fun.

**The insight he keeps returning to:** the value is not 'which form do I fill in', it is getting new hires linked to someone who 'has been there, got the t-shirt'. Veterans (leadership included) hold the tacit knowledge, the culture, and the customer/partner war stories no onboarding doc captures. A random chat is fun but adds little, so **defined goals are essential**.

**Design choices in the idea:**
- Cross-pair deliberately, not just like-for-like: technicians with sales reps and vice versa, leadership with a new social-media hire. Each side learns something.
- Frame it as **employee success, not customer success.**
- Scope is not only the employer: certifications, training paths, language learning, personal-development and productivity advice all count. Personal growth benefits the company.
- Not a replacement for onboarding; could become part of it. Distinct from existing culture-club / informal-talk initiatives, which he judges well-received but unstructured and under-supported.

**Mechanics / sizing:**
- Match formats can be one-to-one or group mentoring; rotate pairings; run a buddy system.
- Set this up per vertical: sales, technical support, marketing, etc.
- <50 participants: match manually, no software. >50: use matching software (or repurpose an existing CRM/marketing tool to link mentors to mentees).
- **Track progress before and after** with targeted survey questions.

**What it needs:** leadership buy-in, promotional material from marketing, and genuine interest from employees. Cost is mainly time, not money (money may come later, e.g. a small learning budget per mentee).

**Steps:** structure the program -> get the word out and ask who's interested -> onboard mentors and mentees -> match pairs -> set goals -> plan and meet -> track progress.

Reusable beyond Nerdio: a lightweight, goal-driven, cross-functional mentoring blueprint for any scaling company.
