# The IT-Doolhof: Salomon's Transition-Management Differentiator

A Salomon customer case framed around a real client quote: "We're in an IT maze and don't know how to get out." Bas notes many organisations feel the same: developments spring up "like mushrooms," and the IT-responsible people are already fully occupied keeping current IT running, which turns into internal debate about resources and cost.

The client's situation showed a recognisable cluster of pain points:

- An own but (heavily) outdated IT infrastructure
- 'Cloudwatervrees' (fear of the cloud) but a genuine need for it
- A sub-optimal relationship with the existing IT partner
- No, or insufficient, IT policy and strategy
- No view of the security risks in their organisation and infrastructure
- No idea which steps to take or what investments they required

Salomon's approach and the concrete goals reached (with customer commitment): a new workplace fitting users' needs; cloud components used in line with functional wishes; better agreements with the existing IT supplier; a foundation for IT policy and information-security policy; insight into risks and mitigating measures; and a roadmap based on logical sequence and urgency.

The **differentiator**: through transition management Salomon takes the reins over projects, negotiations and results, and crucially works *alongside* both the customer and the IT supplier. Because Salomon can sit next to (not against) the incumbent supplier, better mutual agreements get made and "new energy is released on both sides that had disappeared over the years." Salomon then keeps returning periodically to safeguard progress and innovation. He closes by giving Henry Ford the last word.
