# Bas's product/partner inventory and selection process (Detron, 2017)

The sub-process Bas owned to get from an idea to a product list of at most 3 candidate partners/products (primary, second, third choice), feeding the advice report and the overarching proposition-development process. Not every proposition needs a hardware/software product.

Steps:
1. **Idea/service** (described in the proposition-development process). TB gives a functional description, optional brief SWOT, informs the SB.
2. **Pre-selection** to a list of possible partners/products. The list is rarely final and can come from many sources: talking to (ex-)colleagues and third parties, internet research (Google), trade journals, conferences/presentations. Candidates can also come from Detron's existing portfolio, often preferred for strategic reasons.
3. **Appointments** scheduled with suppliers for demos and explanation; Detron's moment to ask questions.
4. **Feedback** gathered by the attending Detron staff, both immediately after the meeting and via the separate feedback form.
5. **First impression** (first conclusion) from all feedback: pluses/minuses, what a supplier would need to change to continue. First suppliers may drop out here. A product matrix can be built to compare suppliers functionally.
6. **Selection** against three criteria sets, each scored 1-5 (1 = poor, 5 = very good): technical (very product-specific), commercial (small changes), operational (small changes). A PoC is an option (more time). Selection can take several weeks.
7. **Results** — the scores get 'logica' plus other findings added, then discussed as a group. The 'logica' is questions/statements that can't easily be scored 1-5; PoC findings and a degree of gut feel matter at least as much. Then the **decision** on which products/suppliers to proceed with.
8. **Product list** — sometimes multiple, sometimes a single partner (IBM BigFix/In-Control example), distinguishing primary vs secondary products; then a high-level draft proposition/advice report for the SB.

The scoring design: quantify what can be quantified (1-5 across technical/commercial/operational), but explicitly carve out a 'logica' bucket for what numbers can't capture, and weight PoC findings and gut feel alongside the scores.
