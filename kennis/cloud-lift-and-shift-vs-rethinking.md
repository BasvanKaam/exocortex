# Cloud Value Comes From Rethinking, Not Lift-And-Shift

Bas's core proposition, titled with the proverb: 'as je doet wat je altijd al deed, krijg je wat je altijd al kreeg' (if you keep doing what you always did, you get what you always got).

**The myth.** Most cloud articles fixate on Office 365, better collaboration, pay-for-what-you-use, infinite scalability, OpEx, and 'no upfront investment'. These are real, eye-catching benefits, but they describe only the easy on-ramp.

**The trap.** The common approach is to pick up what you already run in your own datacenter and rebuild it as-is in some cloud. It looks obvious and you cover ground fast, but appearances deceive. Beyond simple use-cases (sharing documents/data, serving apps or a desktop, 'klik, klik, klaar'), you hit databases, Linux systems, web/file servers, network components, remote access, and the pile of traditional in-house-written Windows applications that are critical to daily operations. You can rebuild all of it in the cloud, easily and largely automated, but the result is a mountain of maintenance-heavy virtual machines. You still pay for upfront work, you don't save on cost, and day-to-day maintenance barely changes; you only gain easier scaling.

**Where the real power is.** It shows only when you move to Serverless, Platform as a Service (PaaS), Infrastructure as Code, REST APIs, microservices and containers, things rarely written about. The cloud is not just a different way of working; it demands a different way of thinking and a fundamentally different approach to (re)building what you already have. Most of your infrastructure disappears, replaced by Serverless, IaC and PaaS, you stop noticing it.

**The economics.** Short-term this needs an investment of both time and money, and the 'no upfront investment' promise no longer holds. Long- to mid-term it returns nothing but advantages and substantial savings. With Serverless you are charged only when code actually runs, and the more customers use a service the cheaper it gets, so it's pay-for-what-you-use, but done differently.

**Evidence he cites.** A Cloud Guru built its streaming training platform entirely on Serverless and IaC with impressive savings/efficiency; Netflix streams nearly 700 hours of film per minute per day and relies largely on microservices, Serverless and PaaS. Size doesn't matter; the required upfront investment does.

**The honest caveat.** In practice this approach is still rare and not every IT environment lends itself 100% to it. The blocker is mindset: too much traditional IT thinking slows adoption. Lack of expertise is stumbling block number one, and re-designing, rebuilding, possibly rewriting and reconfiguring existing systems, apps, storage and networking takes real effort at multiple levels.
