# Key-person dependency on automation is a business risk

Bas's stance: relying on a couple of tech-savvy senior admins to hand-build and maintain your WVD/Azure automation is "putting all your eggs in one basket."

Those admins will run it as smoothly as possible, handling code, templates, updates, daily tasks. But what happens when something breaks and no one is reachable, they are putting out fires elsewhere, they leave the company, or fall ill for a longer period?

The usual escape routes don't fully solve it:
- **Training other employees** isn't straightforward. Doing the job because you're told to is different from doing it from passion, and the cross-training takes real time, especially if it doesn't come naturally.
- **Hiring in expertise** is more expensive, the contractor won't know your company the way your own people did, and per default they leave too. A vicious cycle.

His position: managing IT resources (WVD or otherwise) should not require special skills or a long learning curve. Tooling should reduce time-to-market, spread the workload evenly across a team, relieve individuals of stress, and free up hours per week for more interesting or important work. "Do more with less."
