# Strengthen the community: co-build the Citrix-to-Nerdio course, then turn members into authors

Bas's thinking on how to deepen the community approach around the Citrix-to-Nerdio course. The exact execution is deliberately left open; this captures the direction and the why.

## Why the community is the lever
The people in the community rarely live inside a single vendor. They run Citrix, AVD, Windows 365 and Intune side by side, they see what is actually happening in the field, and they know the material deeply. Many are also looking for new ways to prove what they know, because the familiar certification routes feel stale and the Citrix side has gone quiet on that front. There is real appetite for something fresh, and goodwill to help build it.

## Part 1: co-build the course, give it away
Do not build the Citrix-to-Nerdio course behind closed doors and ship it as a finished product. Bring the community in from day one and offer it free.
- Co-create it: share the outline early and ask for help (real migration stories, gotchas, "I wish I had known that" moments).
- Make it free: no paywall, no registration wall. Free is how this community spreads things and earns trust and reach.
- Anchor it on Nerdio Compass as the map from legacy VDI to the Windows cloud, so it has a practical spine, not a pile of slides.
- Give them a path: pair it with a lightweight, modern way to show they completed it.
- Build a day around it: a hands-on, MicroHack-style day with a live migration and Q&A, so it becomes an event people show up for, not just a link.

## Part 2: turn contributors into authors (ownership)
The bigger prize is moving people from "I helped a bit" to "I own this." Ownership keeps a community engaged long after launch day. Directions (shape to be decided):
- Make them authors, by name: give individuals real ownership of a module, chapter or recurring cheat sheet, credited up front. This worked with the community book; people show up differently when it is genuinely theirs.
- Run a series they carry: recurring, community-filled content such as real migration stories, how they got off Citrix, incident write-ups and how Nerdio helped, the messy-middle lessons nobody blogs about. Owned and told by a community member; Nerdio produces and amplifies.
- Close the feedback loop visibly: not a suggestion box, but a loop where their input clearly lands in the product and the course, and they can see it land.
- Give ownership a path: contributor, author, maintainer, tied to the recognition the community is asking for. An on-ramp, not a closed club.

Strongest version probably combines the first two: a small group of named authors who each own a piece of the course and a story in the series, with the feedback loop underneath.

## Who shapes it
Dave is the right person to shape this with. He talks to these people the most and is already in meetings with them, so he will know who wants ownership, what they would enjoy carrying, and where the appetite is. Get his read early and let him help pick the first authors.
