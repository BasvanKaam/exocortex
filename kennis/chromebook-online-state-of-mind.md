# Chromebooks require an online state of mind

How it worked then (2014): Chromebooks run Chrome OS, essentially an advanced version of the Chrome browser, from which you open and edit documents, manage apps and collaborate. Local compute and SSD-like storage (16 to 64 GB) are minimal because most work happens online and is saved to Google Drive (up to ~100 GB free for the first two years). They are extremely safe, easy to use, lightweight, low-maintenance and cheap, dropping TCO. The trade-off: they are not meant to replace laptops or desktops. If you need locally installed Windows applications, offline use, or the ability to install your own apps, a Chromebook is not sufficient. In an enterprise you connect back to a server-based-computing (SBC/RDS) back-end via a Receiver-style client.
