# How Bas structures a research/advisory report (onderzoeksrapport)

When a client knows it wants to move (e.g. adopt a new platform) but does not yet know how, where to start, what it needs, or what it costs, Bas does not jump to a design. He writes a research/advisory report ("onderzoeksrapport"): a document that makes the organizational and financial impact visible and names the steps and points of attention, so the client can decide whether and how to proceed into a next phase.

The recurring skeleton he uses:

1. **Document control block** - title, author, version, sensitivity ("Vertrouwelijk"), date, and a named internal review action. Versions are explicit: a 0.9 concept goes out for client feedback and presentation, a 1.0 follows after that feedback is folded in.
2. **Introduction** - goal of the research (one or two sentences), approach of the research, and a distribution clause ("need to know" internally; externally only to the commissioning party; no onward distribution without approval).
3. **Management summary** - what the client does, the trigger/occasion ("Aanleiding"), and why the question landed on his desk.
4. **Approach / assessment interviews** - who was interviewed and the question set (see his interview method note).
5. **Technical dependencies** - current situation high-level, then minimum requirements per component, each closed with a "conclusion and next steps".
6. **Organizational** - governance/lifecycle, adoption, training and guidance.
7. **Open questions** - the decisions the client still has to make, listed explicitly, often as either/or choices.
8. **A possible scenario** - a concrete "current vs soon vs change/impact" table so non-technical readers can picture the future way of working.
9. **Phased approach** + **totals including cost estimate** + expected lead time.

Two framing rules he applies hard:

- **"This is a report, not a quote."** He states explicitly that figures and hours are estimates meant to convey impact, that no rights can be derived from them, and that detailed implementation steps belong to a later phase because multiple scenarios are still possible. This keeps the door open and avoids over-promising.
- **Scope is stated up front and defended.** Anything outside the agreed assignment (e.g. evaluating alternatives to the existing platform, a full application/data inventory) is named and pushed to a later phase rather than silently skipped.

The report is deliberately decision-enabling, not a finished design: its job is to make clear where attention is needed next, roughly how long it takes, and what investment to plan for.

*Bron: geanonimiseerde aanpak uit een Salomon-IT klantopdracht (a shared-service organization for municipal real estate and sports facilities (joint venture with municipalities)).*
