# Home-Working Policy and Device Standards

Common failure pattern when home working is suddenly imposed: there is no home-working policy, so employees do not know what is allowed or even possible. Symptoms:
- Budget exists to buy home-working equipment, but no specification of what the equipment must meet, so the helpdesk is flooded trying to make it work.
- People want their two office monitors at home too, with no plan for it.
- Part of the required software turns out to be installed only locally on the fixed office workstation.

What to do:
- Make agreements that are clear to everyone. Decide what the organization will or will not allow regarding its data (classify, GDPR/AVG), assign responsibilities, and set home-working 'rules of the game', e.g. around budgets.
- Let employees choose from approved equipment, in consultation with IT or an external advisor.
- Define the minimum security a home workstation must meet, and how you will verify and keep monitoring it (endpoint management).

There may be little time to think about this up front, which everyone understands, but that does not mean it cannot or should not still be tackled once everyone is working from home.
