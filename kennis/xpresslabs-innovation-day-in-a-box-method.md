# XpressLabs: Bas's Innovation-Day-in-a-box teaching method

Bas took the Nerdio Innovation Days he ran and "boxed" them into a repeatable, self-contained partner enablement format. This note captures the reusable **instructional method**, not the product content.

## The format
- Level 2-300, for new and existing partners, technical hands-on with Nerdio.
- Duration 4-5 hours (a morning or afternoon), flex up or down by partner.
- 16 hands-on lab assignments in a fixed environment (NerdioLab1).
- Optional microhack competition at the end if time permits.
- Outcome by design: each attendee leaves with a working PoC.

## Core teaching principles
1. **Logical sequence / spaced application.** Every step builds on a previous one. Concepts introduced early (e.g. scripted action groups, custom views, hostpool properties) are deliberately applied again later. Repetition is treated as a feature, not filler.
2. **WWW = While We Wait.** Bas's signature device. Whenever a lab kicks off a long-running action (or even when there is no real wait), he fills the gap with a short lecture on something that will be applied later. It turns dead time into seeding-the-next-concept time. The term is used even when nothing is actually pending.
3. **Demonstrate first, then click along.** Each lab is demoed by the instructor first, and/or attendees click along live while he narrates what he is doing. No silent worksheets.
4. **State the contract up front.** Open by discussing goals of the day and expected outcomes ("by the end you will understand X, you will be able to do X"), then walk the agenda. He narrates the plan continuously ("once we do this, we'll have some time, so we'll look at this").
5. **Tie to certification.** Some material maps to the Nerdio NME-200 exam; the session ends by navigating to the exam curriculum and the voucher.

## The lab arc (sequence logic, not the product detail)
Install and harden Nerdio, navigate the support portal, GUI tour, image import, resource rules and quota, hostpool creation, autoscale (create then apply), scripted action groups, custom views, post-patch-Tuesday image/reimage schedules so attendees see the full automated flow, client install and logon, UAM app install via deployment policy, exam curriculum, then a teardown lab that proves resources can't be accidentally deleted.

## The microhack close
A short competition built from the day's content: assemble a configuration or a Model to a given set of prerequisites. First to complete and execute correctly wins a small prize (a drone, a box of Lego). Done on a shared screen so everyone watches.

## Close: review and reflect
A brief resume of what the day taught, the exercises done, and the result (PoC ready), then feedback and questions. Reflection is also applied mid-session after certain labs, on purpose, for repetition.

This whole structure is reusable as Bas's template for any hands-on technical enablement, independent of Nerdio.
