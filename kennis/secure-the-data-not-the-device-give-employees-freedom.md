# Secure the data and access, not the device, and give employees freedom

Bas's reframe of an endpoint-management debate (sparked by a podcast asking why MacBooks aren't more common in Enterprise, with the stock answer being that you can't lock them down like Windows machines). His take: that's the wrong question. The real question is why an employer would want to restrict what employees can do at all. By his read of the trends, most employees want far more freedom than corporate IT currently grants, the private/work boundary is blurring, and multi-location work is now normal.

If you take just those two requirements (freedom plus work-from-anywhere), you are already talking about a mobile device that lives on the internet rather than the internal network. Add the freedom to install your own apps and the logical move is to stop trying to 100% secure the device and instead secure the application, the data and the access to them. In a hybrid world (a mix of SaaS from the cloud and apps from your own datacenter) device-centric lockdown makes even less sense; an endpoint is always needed, so let it be one the user is happy with. MacBooks in Enterprise then become a non-issue.

He ties this to consumerization of IT: people want the same try-it/delete-it ease they have with phone apps on the work floor, which boosts efficiency and satisfaction (a 'win, win, win'). The underlying principle is his constant one: IT is a means, not an end, and is too often treated as a necessary evil; listen to the employee instead of letting IT dictate what they get and how they work.
