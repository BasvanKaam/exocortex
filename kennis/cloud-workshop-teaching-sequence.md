# How Bas sequences a cloud workshop (his facilitation script)

From Bas's own workshop notes, the running order he uses to teach cloud to a mixed business/IT audience:

1. NIST definition - write it out, add the original cloud 'promise', then immediately put it up for debate.
2. Cloud status / state of the market - volumes, statistics, providers, primary differences, with provocative statements added.
3. 'Headache top 5' and the geopolitical angle (China, India, the Gulf states; the 'strijd'/battle between providers).
4. The 'conscience question': should it be about Business Technology rather than IT? Run his IT-vs-BIT scoring assessment to expose how well IT and the business actually cooperate.
5. Deployment models (quick pass; community model is the least-known, give a practical example for public and private).
6. Delivery models IaaS/PaaS/SaaS - emphasise pros/cons; left-to-right the lock-in grows; framed as IaaS = host, PaaS = build, SaaS = consume.
7. Cloud mindset - product-to-service, ownership-to-use; shadow IT and its dangers; role of CIO/IT; cultural shifts; the IT-business gap; IT as internal broker; from management to orchestration ('van beheer naar regie').
8. Cloud economics - Capex vs Opex; his worked financial business case (see separate note).
9. Lift-and-shift and the 6 R's (rehost, replatform, repurchase, refactor, retire, retain) applied to apps/datasets/systems.
10. Cloud architectures and services - datacenter layout, regions, virtual networks, availability sets, fault/update domains, simply explained with redundancy as the focus; RPO/RTO; DR; cloud-native (IaC, serverless, containers); the internet as the ultimate dependency; enterprise architecture as a discipline; the digital workplace / DaaS (incl. WVD).
11. Data, data, data - internet-minute opener, AVG/GDPR, governance, then cloud maturity ('where are you now?').
12. Security - shared responsibility model, zero trust, budgets, threat trends, risk identification, CSA/CISA as sources.
13. Exit strategy - 'the insurance you'd rather not take out but are glad you have.'
14. Trends and future - edge computing, containers, open source, the 'IT professional 2.0', datacenters hitting their limits, rising adoption programmes, BYOD.
15. Conclusion plus questions.

Throughout, the device is to ask the audience first and write along ('meeschrijven'), making the group the source of material.
