# Bas's case for why deep FMA knowledge still matters in the cloud era

In the 'relevance' chapter Bas argues, against the cloud hype, that deep XenApp/XenDesktop FMA knowledge remains valuable. His reasoning:

- Cloud is not one-size-fits-all: you must first define which service model you mean (SaaS, DaaS, IaaS, PaaS) before debating pros and cons.
- With IaaS and PaaS you still install, configure, manage and troubleshoot XenApp/XenDesktop, the OS, data and apps yourself. Even Citrix Cloud needs ongoing monitoring and configuration.
- When something breaks, you still need to troubleshoot, and that requires understanding how components and services interact, hybrid setups included.
- His prediction: he does NOT believe most companies will move their entire on-prem datacentres to the cloud within 5-10 years. Hybrid solutions are the way forward, and on-prem RDSH/VDI (FMA) architectures are 'here to stay for at least another five to ten years, if not longer (my guess is longer).'

He acknowledges he sounds negative about cloud but insists that is not his intent: the cloud has just as many, if not more, advantages, and tens of thousands of companies have already migrated. His point is only that the full cloud package is 'not for everybody'.
