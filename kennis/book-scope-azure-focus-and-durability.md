# Scope of the book: Azure-leaning, business-plus-tech, durable basics

Bas sets clear expectations for the book. Although all three of the largest cloud providers are covered, most practical examples target Microsoft Azure, because Azure is the platform he encounters most in his daily work. Primary differences and characteristics of the other providers are still discussed.

The book is deliberately NOT a deep technical manual on designing networks, landing zones, backup strategies, firewall configuration, or VM types; he says enough excellent books already exist on those. Architectures are discussed in some depth but that is not the main goal. It is a practical guide to what cloud means and the impact it has on business operations, cultural shifts and process changes.

Each chapter has clear explanations, examples, images, practice cases, ten to fifteen written key takeaways, practical assignments and important questions. Topics include Total Cost of Ownership (TCO), Return On Investment (ROI), building a valid cloud business case (which is about more than money), the cloud economy, organisational change, principles, architectures, pitfalls, Shadow IT (and how to profit from it), IT as an internal broker, the move from knowledge to orchestration ("van kennis naar regie"), and a cloud exit strategy.

Bas's promise: although cloud develops fast, the basics remain unchanged, so most of the book will still be relevant and helpful in 5 or 10 years.
