# Call them traditional Windows apps, not legacy

Bas's position, grounded in research he did for a paper on cloud-based computing: millions of native Windows applications are still in use by thousands of companies worldwide. Rather than calling them 'legacy', it is more appropriate to label them 'traditional'.

The argument:
- There is a misconception about how prevalent web and SaaS apps really are. Despite the abundance of options, most companies still heavily rely on traditional Windows applications, some running over a thousand of them.
- Replacing or transforming even a single app with a SaaS/web alternative can take weeks or months; doing it at scale would take many years, and there is the unresolved question of what happens to the associated data.
- Therefore AVD plays a crucial role in keeping these traditional applications alive, letting companies keep serving users while modernizing where possible. Modernization cannot happen overnight, and AVD ensures users keep working without interruption in the meantime.
- He pushes back on AVD critics directly: 'Despite criticism from some, Azure Virtual Desktop (AVD) plays a crucial role in keeping these traditional applications alive.'
