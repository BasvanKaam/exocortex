# Bas's proposition development process (Detron, 2017)

The overarching process Bas owned as Detron Technology Officer for getting from an idea to a fully worked-out proposition. The Technology Board (TB) acts as advisory body to the Solutions Board (SB); the advice report is one of the most important deliverables. Input is an idea for a new service/proposition; output is an advice report / draft proposition followed by SB approval, after which the proposition is detailed out.

The 11 steps:
1. **Idea/service** arises — from a (preferably repeated) customer question, market developments, a colleague, or the TB/SB itself.
2. **TB** ideally sees the idea first, with a clear motivation (via the proposition idea form). TB gives it a functional description, optionally a brief SWOT, then informs the SB.
3. **Core?** Decide whether the idea fits Detron's core business (one of the four work domains).
   - If no: the originating Business Unit may take ownership; the SB decides, the TB supports/advises.
4. **TB & SB leading** if yes. Before moving to inventory, the first 'feel' must be good: broadly positive response, a sense the proposition has a more-than-average chance of success.
5. **Inventory** of possible products/partners (often several); covered by a separate process. Candidates can and often should come from Detron's existing portfolio even when the idea came from outside; vendors may pitch.
6. **Selection** against functional, technical, and crucially operational and commercial criteria (criteria differ per category/proposition).
7. **Product list** — sometimes two-plus partners, sometimes a single one (IBM BigFix/In-Control cited as the single-partner example).
8. **Draft proposition / business case** — high-level, built on the info gathered in steps 5 and 6.
9. **Advice to SB** — the advice report (separately described), optionally worked out per product/partner.
10. **Approval** — SB gives go / no-go / hold, may ask for clarification, may send it back for rework against feedback, or reject it outright.
11. **Work out** — after approval, detail the proposition; the TB's role shifts from leading to supporting where needed.

Design principle: TB leads exploration and advice, SB owns the decision and budget, and the TB deliberately steps back to a support role once a proposition is approved.
