# Build a practitioner community as a go-to-market motion

Bas's reusable idea: stand up a vendor-adjacent practitioner community (the Nerdio Tech Insider program) as a low-pressure way to better serve customers and partners and to create a two-way feedback loop. The design principles he applies:

- **Ask for almost nothing in return.** The pitch is explicit: "I do not expect anything from you... we appreciate any feedback, suggestion, feature requests." Lowering the ask raises the join rate.
- **Personal invitations first**, then a public announcement. Warm-start the community with hand-picked members before opening the doors.
- **Don't build your own island.** Reuse an existing, credible community space (here, the IGEL EUC community Slack, getting a dedicated channel) rather than forcing people into yet another standalone tool.
- **Give the community real value to justify the ask**: training webinars and sessions, swag, access to engineering and sales, a heads-up newsletter, a feature-request channel, and early sight of releases and selected roadmap items.
- **Anchor it on a recurring schedule**: a Meetup page as the public home for webinars, training, and virtual get-togethers, plus a launch/kick-off event to seed momentum.
- **Spin up the content engine alongside the community**: intro blog, KB article (reusing blog copy), launch webinar, follow-on webinar series.

This is the field-CTO playbook: community as content distribution, feedback funnel, and relationship layer all at once.
