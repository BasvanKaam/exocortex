# A Learning Company Should Apply Learning to Its Own People

Bas's stance: a company that sells itself as the example of an always-learning organization should turn that principle inward and invest deliberately in developing its own staff, not just its customers. Informal, well-meaning internal talks are not enough; without structure and a plan they do not get the attention they deserve. Mentoring should be goal-driven and tracked, framed as employee success, and the cost is time rather than budget. The personal growth of an individual is itself a return for the company.
