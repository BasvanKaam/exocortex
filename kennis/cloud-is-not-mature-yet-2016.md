# The cloud is taking over, but in 2016 it is not yet proven for everybody

In August 2016, against a LinkedIn/Twitter timeline treating cloud as the holy grail, Bas takes a measured contrarian stance. He's explicit it's not cloud-bashing (he's studying Azure architecture, exam included, and loves the Citrix/Microsoft innovation), but he pushes back on the general assumption that within 3-5 years most organisations will have moved their datacenters, desktops, apps and data into the cloud. His prediction: it could take 10+ years to get most companies there, if at all -- he points out virtualization took 20+ years and architects still argue physical vs virtual, and that containers, hyperconvergence and app layering are years old yet only a few percent actually use them. Acknowledges AWS and Azure's huge growth (AWS up 58% YoY, Azure up 120%) while insisting large-scale IaaS/PaaS is still not seen as proven by many. The core take: move to the cloud for the right reason and the problem it solves, not because it's cheaper or trendy.
