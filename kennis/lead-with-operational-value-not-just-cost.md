# Lead with operational and business value; cost savings is the cherry on top

Bas argues the story around Nerdio (and tooling like it) should foreground the operational and business benefits — time saved, fewer manual errors, lower risk, added efficiency, FTE freed up for more strategic work — with raw Azure cost savings as the cherry on top rather than the whole pitch.

His reasoning: the product became well known for cost savings, and rightly so, but a story built only on dollars is fragile. Many customers still fixate only on the money they'll save, so you have to become more creative and broaden the narrative. He frames operational time savings (versus doing things natively or via IaC) translated into hours, then into money at an hourly rate, then into FTE equivalents and an efficiency percentage, as an additional tool in the toolbox to strengthen the case. Decisions ultimately come down to total cost of ownership, not headline infrastructure savings.
