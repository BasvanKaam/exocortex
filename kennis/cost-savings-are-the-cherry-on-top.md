# Cost savings are the cherry on top; business value and continuity matter more

Bas's central argument: Nerdio earned its reputation as a cost saver (he cites 50-70% on compute and storage) and that still holds, but he deliberately reframes the conversation around total ROI and business value, of which cost savings are only a small part – "the cherry on top."

His test for the reader: compare 50-70% cost savings against scenarios like a Monday-morning Azure regional outage where employees cannot log in at all. Which matters more? His answer is business continuity, and his point is that with Nerdio you do not have to choose; you get business value, continuation, and cost savings together.

On continuity specifically he points to: automated regular backups of critical data/systems; DRaaS that splits data/systems into an alternative region for rapid failover; and the ability to specify additional fallback VM sizes/types so host creation, auto-scale, auto-grow and auto-heal do not fail when Azure compute capacity is exhausted for the preferred size in a region.

He also stresses that conversations still revolve around 'features', but the real job is translating those features into added value, with lower cost as just one outcome.
