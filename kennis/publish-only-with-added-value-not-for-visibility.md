# Publish only when there is something to say, not to stay visible

Stance from the salomon-it.blog launch post (2018). Bas rejects fixed publishing cadences as an end in themselves. Most blogs ship on a schedule (every two weeks, monthly, weekly) to keep the author visible; he argues that publishing purely to be seen is worthless. A post should only go out when there is genuinely something to report and it delivers added value to the reader.

Paired with this is a brevity discipline: Salomon blogs run roughly 400-450 words or fewer, are written primarily in Dutch, and take about 1.5 to 2 minutes to read. Longer subjects get split across multiple parts or become a separate whitepaper. He frames brevity through TL;DR / TLDR culture and an Einstein maxim about simple explanation. The deeper claim: a clear message beats long introductions and detail-dumps, and writing short also lowers the barrier to actually writing.

This is a sharper, value-gated version of the throughput-driven short-form thinking behind his earlier '-1000' blog series (basvankaam.com, 2015): same brevity instinct, but here the trigger to publish is added value, not visibility or cadence.
