# IGEL is a software company, not just a thin-client maker

Bas's recurring 2018 thesis on IGEL: most people he talks to still pigeonhole IGEL as "a thin client company," which he concedes is fair given their history, but argues is now outdated. His framing: "Nowadays it's all about the bits and bytes." He explicitly aligns with IGEL's own self-description that they are a software company more than anything else ("I agree"). The ROI angle is the practical hook he keeps returning to: software like UDC3 and UD Pocket gives old desktops, laptops, tablets and even old thin clients "a second chance at life." This is an early, openly partner-friendly stance, consistent with his Liquidware and other vendor advocacy of this period.
