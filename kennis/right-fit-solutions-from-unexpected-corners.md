# The Right Fix Can Come From an Unexpected Corner

Bas's framing of the Windows 7 end-of-support problem is characteristically pragmatic and option-oriented rather than alarmist. Instead of pushing a forced rush to Windows 10, he validates the many legitimate reasons an organization might want to keep Windows 7 running for some users: an app set tied to Windows 7, no time or budget to migrate, or being mid hybrid-cloud transition.

He then presents the Windows Virtual Desktop as 'a solution from an unexpected corner', meeting the client where they are rather than demanding they conform to the latest platform. This continues his recurring stance that you pick what fits the client's actual situation and constraints, not the textbook ideal. The treadmill of mandatory Windows 10 updates is treated as a real, sympathetic burden ('volkomen begrijpelijk'), not a failure of the customer.
