# Method: Agree PoC Success Criteria Up Front

Bas's repeatable way of running a proof-of-concept so it converts. The principle is channel-agnostic: define what "done well" means before you start, then instrument against it.

## The principle
Always agree explicit success criteria with the customer before the PoC starts: when is the PoC successful, what do they want out of it at a minimum. His field observation: when those agreed goals are met, the odds of going to production are very high, around 90%.

## Structure the criteria
- Split into **critical needs** (must work) and **like-to-have** (nice if available). This separation keeps the go/no-go decision clean.
- Define **how success is measured**: are critical items working and reporting, how many like-to-haves are available, is the tool delivering the wanted functionality, are the expected savings showing up.
- Name **resources aligned** (who measures each goal) and **decision makers** (who must be engaged to buy) explicitly.

## Cadence beats a single demo
Set a fixed time frame (e.g. 30 days) and plan one or two follow-up sessions across it. This gives the client room to ask questions in between and lets you monitor progress against the agreed criteria. A worked timeline: Day 1 deploy, Day 5-7 health check, Day 14 sync on success measurements, Day 21 decision-makers review, Day ~29 final wrap-up and next steps.

## Then personalize
Once finalized and agreed, personalize the criteria document and send it back to the customer so it's a shared, owned artifact — not a vendor checklist.
