# Citrix's problem is integration and focus, not lack of products

Stance (September 2015, written as the Elliott Management activist pressure on Citrix was playing out): Bas's core argument is that Citrix already has the pieces (Scout/TaaS, Director, Concierge, monitoring heritage from EdgeSight) but fails to stitch them together. His recurring move is 'make it part of Director / Studio': fold Scout and TaaS into Director, give Concierge a real helpdesk ticketing role across XenApp/XenDesktop, bring back EdgeSight-grade monitoring owned by Citrix so it controls the stack top to bottom.

On the Elliott affair specifically: he sort of agreed at one point but not with getting rid of NetScaler. His framing is that it's all about prioritising and focus. 'It doesn't really matter if they sell off the GoTo suite, develop another mouse or make even more lights flash, it's about focus and giving each product the attention it needs.' If Citrix does sell products, he trusts they thought it through. Notably optimistic and deferential to the company and to Mark Templeton ('Mark T') personally.

He also flags the Universal Print Server criticism sharply: Citrix marketed UPS compression as an enterprise feature for remote-branch printing, then admitted in its own blog that compression didn't deliver in the real world, tested only at small scale. His verdict: 'I think this approach isn't helping Citrix.'
