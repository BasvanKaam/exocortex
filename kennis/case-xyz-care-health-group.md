# Fictional case: XYZ-Care Health Group

The book's running fictional case is XYZ-Care Health Group, a leading Dutch healthcare organisation serving more than 100,000 patients per year, with 5,000 employees across 12 locations and a growing home-care (thuiszorg) offering. Management has decided to consider a cloud migration, supported by SCAF.

The org structure includes a CIO leading IT (infrastructure, application management, security teams), care-delivery departments with afdelingshoofden and zorgmanagers, home-care staff on mobile devices, Finance/Admin with a CFO judging budgets and ROI, HR & Training, and a Data-analyse & Innovatie team.

Current workplace: a mix of desktop PCs (fixed locations) and laptops (management and home care), with four pain points, manual local maintenance/patching, security risk on laptops used outside the network (encryption and MFA not standard), inconsistent access to the EPD and planning tool on aging hardware, and high device costs.

Application landscape: standard Office apps (fine), plus the core EPD running on-premises with a hard link to an older database; a SaaS planning tool with periodic sync problems; an on-premises HRM system needing modernisation; an aging financial management system; and a poorly scalable reporting tool. Multiple dependencies exist (EPD-reporting, planning tool pulling from EPD, financial system fed by HRM, HRM feeding EPD with rosters/roles).

Future vision: XYZ-Care is considering Azure Virtual Desktop (AVD) for uniform access regardless of location or device, built-in security (MFA, data encryption), cost reduction via centralisation, and scalability. A recurring sub-theme is the gap between IT and the business causing miscommunication.
