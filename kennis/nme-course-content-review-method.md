# How Bas reviews Nerdio L&D content as SME

Bas's instructional-review approach, visible across his NME-201 feedback, reusable as a method:

- Lead with the learner's value perception. He flags an opening overview module that "doesn't add much" and proposes cutting it in favor of the stronger core-value sub-module that follows, rather than keeping a weak intro for completeness.
- Demand a complete value statement. Where a benefits list feels light, he suggests enriching it (even noting a GPT prompt could surface the most common unique selling points) so the core message isn't undersold.
- Fix naming to match reality. He pushes to rename a 'prerequisites' module to '(API) permissions' because most permissions are applied during installation, not beforehand, so the label misleads learners.
- Tie training to real field pain. He repeatedly maps modules to recurring real-world friction (permissions, security hardening, install methods) and proposes pulling in field decks the Enterprise/SE team already use as a starting point, then flags where a topic deserves a deeper, more advanced follow-on lesson.
- Reduce fear, uncertainty and doubt. He wants permission topics to explain why a permission is needed and what happens to it afterwards, to take away FUD.
- Mind look-and-feel and first impressions. He critiques clips that all open on the least attractive dashboard and suggests starting from a more visual page.
- Validate every knowledge-check and exam question against the truth. He hunts for wrong 'correct' answers, duplicate answer options, and questions testing material not taught in the module.

The throughline: training must be technically accurate, honestly named, anchored in real deployment experience, and continuously verified question-by-question.
