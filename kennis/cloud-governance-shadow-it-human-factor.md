# Governance, shadow IT, and the human factor in cloud adoption

Bas's view on the non-technical blockers, which he treats as the most dangerous:

- The real danger isn't where to start (training, external advice, pilots, starting small all help, they just slow things down), it's **governance, or the lack of it**, combined with the deceptive simplicity cloud appears to offer.
- Skills gap is structural: 32% reported missing critical cloud knowledge; 49% admitted they lack the skills to design, implement, and maintain a working cloud environment even while 91% invest in cloud training (Nutanix, 2019).
- Cloud's simplicity invites **Shadow IT**: users spin up free/paid services for business use without grasping the impact. Someone eventually has to own them, ideally internal IT, because no one understands the business, processes, and risks better. Until governance is in place, it's wise to limit or postpone cloud usage to avoid disappointment and protect internal trust.
- **The human factor / loss of control:** even when control isn't technically lost, it often *feels* lost (e.g. Office 365 goes down and IT can only wait for Microsoft; in your own datacenter your team can at least investigate, estimate impact, and report back). Lack of trust shouldn't be underestimated; outages leave a mark and lock-in fears grow.
- **Resistance and culture:** new ways of working meet resistance; cultural shifts are unintended but natural side effects. Break change into manageable steps and keep communication clear. 'Cloud isn't just about technology. In fact, if you ignore the human element, your cloud journey is likely to fail.'

Key reframing: 'Cloud is just a way to deliver IT. It's a tool, a means to an end, not a strategy in and of itself, but part of a broader IT vision.'
