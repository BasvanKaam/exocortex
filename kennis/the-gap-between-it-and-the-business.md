# The Gap Between IT and 'The Business'

Bas describes a fundamental gap in many organisations between IT and the business, from differing priorities, ways of working, and lack of mutual understanding. IT focuses on technological innovation and efficiency; the business on strategy, profitability, and customer focus. The result: miscommunication, delays, inefficiency. Often there's no structural consultation, jargon causes confusion, and knowledge is poorly shared.

Mutual frustrations: IT complains decisions are made without its involvement (causing unclear goals, shifting priorities, and Shadow IT); the business complains projects take too long, cost too much, and have unrealistic expectations.

Bas defines **'the business'**: the people, processes, and products needed to set and realise strategic company goals — often roles like CEO, COO, CFO, CTO, sales, HR, marketing, and department managers, forming the management team (MT) or board. He argues IT solutions should serve the business's strategic objectives, and the business's wishes/requirements should steer the broader strategic IT plan. The key to closing the gap is collaboration — a culture change centred on communication, transparency, and (above all) mutual understanding.
