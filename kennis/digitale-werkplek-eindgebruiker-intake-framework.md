# Digital workplace intake: end-user question framework

A reusable, theme-organized question set for assessing a digital workplace from the **end-user** perspective, used at the start of a workplace-modernization engagement. The goal is to surface lived experience (friction, wait times, satisfaction), not to inventory the technical estate. Questions for management are framed differently; this set is user-facing.

## Open the conversation, don't open the questionnaire
Before any structured round, start broad: "What does your ideal workplace look like?" and "What works well for you today, and what doesn't?" This warms up the respondent and frames the rest as a conversation rather than an interrogation.

## Theme 1 — Mobility and flexibility
How important is it to the user to:
- Have access to their data anytime, anywhere.
- Choose where they work, unbound by time, location, or device.
- Work across devices (laptop, tablet, phone).
- Choose their own device.
- Exchange information with colleagues: co-editing the same document, sharing/sending files securely.
- Meet online, on the road or from the office (split voice-only vs. voice plus video).
- Reach colleagues easily online and see presence/availability.
- Work without worrying about security, and whether that worry is a current hindrance.

## Theme 2 — The digital workplace itself
- Is the workplace (data, applications) easily accessible? How many steps to log in? Two-factor? Soft or hard token? Multiple logins required?
- How long from power-on to actually working (boot, login, first app open)? Aimed at getting a feel for the whole, not a precise benchmark.
- How is that experience perceived, and does it differ between home/remote and office?
- Are there restrictions inside the workplace? Enough freedom of movement to work efficiently? Ask for a concrete example of what they run into.
- Launching apps, accessing data, editing documents: is this smooth, and are load times acceptable? Structural friction usually surfaces on its own; asking directly about load times also works.
- Are all apps and data reachable from one environment/portal (SaaS, web, mobile, locally installed)?

## Theme 3 — Support and requests
- How satisfied is the user with support, and what's the overall feeling?
- Is logging an incident or asking a general question easy?
- Is help competent and professional?
- Are turnaround times acceptable and per agreement?
- Is there a self-service portal (password reset, requesting apps, a new workplace, permissions)?
- Does onboarding a new employee (account, workplace, permissions, apps, access) go smoothly, and how long does it take?

## Theme 4 — Wrap-up / general
- All things considered, rate the current workplace concept 1 to 10.
- What irritates them most?
- How would they fix it themselves?
- What is actually well arranged?
- Anything we forgot, anything they want to add?

## Why it works
The structure moves from aspiration (ideal workplace) through the three pillars users actually feel (mobility, the workplace, support) to a single satisfaction score plus an open floor. The score gives a quotable baseline; "how would you fix it yourself" surfaces requirements without leading the witness.
