# The human factor: loss of control and trust

In his paper Bas argues the human factor often centres on giving up control. Stories of admins keeping cloud services out the door for fear of their jobs are known and can slow adoption, but that's not his point. The real issue: depending on the service, the operations team loses (full) control over systems, data and applications — and even when it doesn't, it *feels* that way.

His example: Office 365 goes down — now what? There's nothing they can do but wait for Microsoft to fix it; nobody can say in advance whether that's ten minutes, an hour or half a day. In your own datacenter you can generally switch fast, send admins at it, and at minimum estimate impact and duration to report to management.

**Trust** — don't underestimate the lack of it. Cloud outages happen with some regularity and scare people. Vendor lock-in plays a growing role ("what if we don't like it?"). Questions about authentication and data security linger even when functionally well-explained: is our data safe, who can access it, are our usernames and passwords secure?

Supporting data, the Nutanix Enterprise Cloud Index (2019) survey of 2,650 IT decision-makers: 73% said they are moving workloads back to on-premises (22% moving five or more — main reasons cost, performance, security/compliance); security is a primary reason to choose hybrid cloud; over a quarter (28%) of IT pros consider the hybrid cloud model the most secure.
