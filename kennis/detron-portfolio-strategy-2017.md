# Detron Portfolio Strategy (Short and Mid-Term, 2017)

Bas's strategy memo as Technology Officer, written to align Detron on a multi-year product and innovation direction. Explicitly NOT split by business unit ("we werken steeds meer toe naar een Detron"), based on his own insight plus conversations with colleagues. He frames the problem clearly: the bottleneck is not a lack of ideas, enthusiasm or initiative, those are abundant; it is focus, follow-through and time.

High-over priorities (his speerpunten):
- Profile Detron as a knowledge house and total-unburdener ("kennishuis en totaal ontzorger"); marketing plays the lead role. The potential is there but the reputation is not yet.
- Get partner/vendor portfolio and the product/service catalog (PDC) under control; fewer point-solutions.
- More room for innovation initiatives (which need not mean new tech).
- Stand up working groups for emerging tech: IoT (industrial / IIoT focus), big data, blockchain, AR, AI.
- Deeper, more specific security focus, up to a dedicated security branch with its own SOC.
- Build Azure knowledge to play integrator in a multi-cloud strategy.
- Reframe cloud as a billing model (Opex) and lean into rental/lease over hardware sale.
- Invest in hybrid (private) cloud: Azure Stack and hyperconverged.
- Build a knowledge matrix (certifications, interests, experience) so the same colleagues aren't always the ones approached.

Concrete short/mid-term SaaS propositions he names: Secure Datashare (rename of an existing ShareFile-based service, market it hard around data-leak/GDPR themes), Backup as a Service, Archiving as a Service ("Triple A"), Storage as a Service (ship a backup unit to seed-load data offline, then incremental sync, to dodge slow-line bottlenecks), DRaaS via Azure rather than own datacenter, Office365 backup, data classification / rights management, GDPR advisory + tooling, Awingu for workspace aggregation.

Market-segmentation ideas: Apple for business (favorable 2-4yr ROI), Secure Datashare for finance/medical, Chromebooks in education tied to WaaS.

Reusable strategic frames he leans on: Lean Startup (validated learning, MVP, build-measure-learn) to shorten time to market; "innovation is often creativity, not new tech" (relaunch/repackage existing services); cloud as integrator play.
