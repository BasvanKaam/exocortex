# Bas's list of reasons companies stay (partly) on-premises

Bas enumerates the practical reasons a company may choose to keep, or only partly move, its datacentre off the cloud. These are his field-experience objections, not vendor talking points:

- Company-sensitive data: where is it stored, who has access.
- Apps separated from their data: not all apps function well when physically separated, and in practice they often are.
- Authentication: many companies do not want user authentication to happen 'in the cloud', even when technically it need not be a problem; a hard one to explain.
- Trust: uptime, security breaches, SLAs, responsibilities, performance and user experience.
- Exit strategy: fear that once you leap into the cloud you cannot undo it, or it will be costly.
- Control: reluctance to rely on a third party for infrastructure, data and apps.
- Internet connections become vital, 'putting all eggs in one basket'.
- Costs: cloud hosting is not cheap (at least not then), and migration takes time, testing and planning.
- Security reframe: bigger cloud providers are NOT more secure by default. They invest more on average, but 'the bigger you are the more interesting you become. And eventually they will find a way.'
- SaaS only gets you so far: legacy Windows apps stick around for years.
- DaaS fits many but is too restrictive/limited for others.

Key takeaways he attaches: leverage the cloud for Burst Capacity and backup; don't forget printing and scanning bandwidth/latency limits when hosting RDSH/VDI in the cloud; start small and go hybrid.
