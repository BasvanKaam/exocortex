# Bas: early business-case documents are deliberately rough and inconsistent

Bas openly flags that he is not 100% consistent across his own example artefacts on purpose. In one table he writes 'Nader uit te werken' (to be worked out) for the back-up line, while in scenario 1 he writes out a full back-up solution. This inconsistency is deliberate: a business case is a first step, a starting point, and these kinds of documents and overviews are subject to regular change during the first weeks.

He also deliberately leaves the cost figures empty in his scenario/cost overview (Scenario 1/2, monthly costs, one-off costs for licences, inventory, implementation). Filling them in wouldn't add value because he doesn't know when the reader picks up the book, and it is explicitly only a first rough estimate ('eerste grove schatting').

The takeaway: don't polish a first business case to perfection. Get something tangible on paper, expect it to churn, and refine as insight grows.
