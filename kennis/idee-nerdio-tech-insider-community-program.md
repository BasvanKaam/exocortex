# Idee: Nerdio Tech Insider Community Program

Bas's model for the Nerdio Tech Insider Program (announced ~2020, around the WVD era): a vendor-owned technical community. It is a reusable template for a no-strings-attached community as a product-feedback and advocacy engine. The core move is to name and formalize the community you already have, anchor it on quality contact and a visible feedback-to-release loop, and keep it explicitly free of sales pressure.

## Premise
- The community is about the members and what the vendor can learn from them, not about selling. Explicitly tech-only: 'No marketing, no sales, leads, or anything like that.' Give tech/community folks what they actually want to see, hear, and play with.
- Ask close to nothing in return except honest feedback from time to time. No upfront commitments, no salesperson pressure.
- Quality over quantity. Start small (a Meetup page), do not chase member counts. If it grows, great; if not, fine. The aim is genuine contact, not scale.

## What the vendor offers members
- Community-focused early release of the software, plus early/hands-on product access.
- A training series on getting started with the product (Nerdio Manager for WVD), and broader training on WVD, EUC, and Azure.
- Regular portfolio/product updates.
- Best-effort support plus access to engineering and sales teams.
- Open discussion in a private Slack channel, plus a dedicated channel for feedback and feature requests.
- Online webinars and technical sessions.
- Occasional NDA roadmap previews and an early 'Heads Up' newsletter so members hear news first.
- A rebate/referral (kickback) program for those interested, and swag; in-person dinner/fun events when feasible.

## Mechanics
- Two-step join: a Meetup page (for RSVPs to events/webinars) and a private Slack channel (for collaboration).
- Launch via a webinar event.
- Co-branding encouraged: members may freely use the community logos on socials, sites, and blog posts.

## Feedback loop
- Bas had already been collecting feedback informally for months before naming the program. Community input (from partners, customers, colleagues, peers, interested parties) feeds the roadmap.
- Monthly software releases make the loop visible: contributors see their suggestions ship.

## Tone of the pitch
- "Come 'Nerd' with us." Get to know members, value their feedback, help in any way (demos, support with prospects).

Reusable lesson: a community works as a low-friction product-shaping loop when you remove the sales motion entirely and let practitioners who talk to hundreds of customers tell you what to improve.
