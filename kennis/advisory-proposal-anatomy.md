# Anatomy of a Fixed-Price Advisory Proposal

The structure Bas uses when turning a first intake conversation into a written offer. Observed on a fixed-price IT-advisory engagement, it is reusable for any scoped consulting deliverable.

**1. Mirror the intake conversation back.** Open by summarizing the situation discussed in the intake meeting, dated and attributed to that meeting. Restate the client's own findings (here: end-of-life platforms, weak backup/restore, too many overlapping vendors, a firefighting IT team, the wish for a roadmap with a stated strategy). This proves you listened and anchors the scope in the client's words before you propose anything.

**2. Separate this scope from adjacent asks.** The client asked for several things; the proposal explicitly names them and then states that this document addresses only one (the assessment leading to the report and roadmap), with the others delivered separately. One proposal, one scope.

**3. The method as numbered steps.** Lay out the work as discrete, sequenced steps so the client can see exactly what they buy and in what order (see the four-step assessment-to-roadmap structure).

**4. Lead time, preconditions, investment.** Give a realistic lead time (here six weeks) with a note that intensity scales up if needed, and a recurring short progress meeting to steer mid-flight. List the preconditions that must be met. State the investment as a clear fixed price, excluding VAT, inclusive of travel, with payment terms (here 50% at start, 50% on completion, 15-day term) and an offer validity window (30 days).

**5. A signed engagement confirmation.** Close with a formal inzetbevestiging naming both parties, the assignment, cost, lead time, payment terms, location, and signature blocks. The proposal and the contract are one document.

The through-line: make scope, sequence, price, and obligations unambiguous on a single page-set, so the client signs knowing exactly what they get and what is expected of them.

*Bron: geanonimiseerde aanpak uit een Salomon-IT klantopdracht (a pharmaceutical company).*
