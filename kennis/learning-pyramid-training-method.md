# Method: The Learning Pyramid Applied to Technical Training

Bas's core L&D framework. He references the Learning Pyramid (often attributed to Edgar Dale) and designs each training format to push toward active learning, where retention is far higher.

Retention by method (as he cites them):
- Reading: 10%
- Listening: 20%
- Watching visuals: 30%
- Demonstrations: 50%
- Group discussions: 70%
- Practical application (hands-on): 80%
- Teaching others: 90%

How he maps training formats to the pyramid:
- **Hands-on labs** — configure, test, troubleshoot in real time on real scenarios. Practical application, ~80% retention.
- **Innovation Days** — collaboration, brainstorming, experimentation; participants articulate their thinking. Group learning, ~70% retention, with the explicit step of aligning insights to organizational goals (a step often forgotten).
- **Partner training** — interactive; participants explain concepts and demonstrate understanding (toward teaching-others, up to ~90%). He keeps it 'fluent' / 'dynamic' — adjustable in depth and topic to the audience: 'it's your time, let's spend it the best we can.'
- **MicroHacks** — microlearning: short, focused bursts with live demos, real examples, non-guided hands-on practice. ~50–80% retention.
- **Presentations** — naturally visual/auditory, but elevated with live demos, real-world examples, and interactive Q&A to lift retention.

His three structural pillars for every session: **Strategy, Execution, Reflection** — start with clear objectives, move into active participation (labs, discussions, demos), and close with reflection (follow-ups, surveys, knowledge checks). Reflection can take just minutes. 'It's through repetition that we learn.'

Knowledge flow-back principle: design so knowledge returns to the organization — participants host internal workshops, session resources become internal documentation, and engaged learners become champions/advocates. Virtual sessions add the record option for reuse.
