# Cloud deployment models (public, private, hybrid, community)

Deployment models describe how and from where Cloud environments are deployed and managed; companies choose based on needs for control, cost and security.

- Publieke Cloud: accessible to the general public (e.g. AWS, Azure), fully managed by external providers, pay-per-use. Good for organisations that do not want to manage infrastructure.
- Private Cloud: used exclusively by one organisation, offering full control over infrastructure and data - important for strict security or compliance requirements. Managed by the organisation itself (often from its own datacenter) or by a third party. Example: Bechtel's Project Services Network (PSN), giving 40,000 employees worldwide access to IT resources via a private Cloud.
- Hybride Cloud: combines private and public, giving the best of both worlds - public flexibility/scalability plus private control/security. Example: Microsoft Azure Local, extending on-premises datacenters with public Cloud services.
- Community Cloud: shared by organisations with similar requirements (e.g. compliance, security), managed by a third party or the organisations themselves. Common in the public sector, e.g. a Shared Service Center (SSC).
