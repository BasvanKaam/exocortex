# Proposal structure: conversation recap plus first impression under reservation

How I structure a first proposal/offer after an intake conversation and site visit.

## The shape
1. **Recap of the conversation** — play back the client's situation, decisions already taken, and the open questions in my own words. This proves I listened and aligns on the starting point before I propose anything.
2. **First impression** — my conclusions, explicitly stated as preliminary ("under some reservation"). These are concrete, technically substantiated observations and early recommendations, not vague reassurance. I name where I see no obstacles and where I see real attention points.
3. **The framework / approach** — the phased approach (the four pillars), tailored to how it would look for this specific client, with the pillars that don't apply explicitly marked as out of scope.
4. **Open the scope I haven't covered yet** — flag what we did not discuss (e.g. end-user impact and adoption) as something for a next session, so nothing is silently assumed in scope.
5. **A dependency/inventory annex** — the concrete checklist the engagement will work against, with a line like "looks like a lot, it isn't" to lower the barrier.
6. **How to proceed** — next steps and who to contact.

## Tone and stance
- Open with genuine thanks for time and the clear explanation; close the same way.
- Substantiate technical claims (license savings, supported distributions, PaaS alternatives) rather than asserting them.
- "The feeling has to be right" is a stated decision criterion, not a throwaway. I say out loud when the gut feel about the fit is good, because that matters in a transition partnership.
- I'm transparent that some things still need verification; I flag rather than guess.
