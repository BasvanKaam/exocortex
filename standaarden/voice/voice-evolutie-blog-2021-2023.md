# 2021-2023: how Bas wrote

This is a thin but telling era: the blog has slowed to a trickle (7 posts, clustered in early 2021 plus two stragglers), and the centre of gravity has shifted away from long Citrix/EUC deep-dives toward podcast announcements (the EUCdigest run), vendor-release write-ups, a personal lifestyle post, and a practical cheat-sheet release. This is his authentic PRE-AI baseline: no scaffolded structure, no banned-word polish, just his natural register.

## Tone
Warm, generous, and reader-first. He writes as a community insider talking to peers, not as a marketer. Even promotional posts feel like a genuine recommendation ("had the honor to chat with", "Find out, everything there is to know about Awingu and why you should try it out"). Helpfulness is the through-line: he frames artifacts as things meant to help you, manages expectations honestly, and invites you to make them better.

## Structure
Short and functional in this era. Podcast posts are near-boilerplate (intro the guest/format, one-line value, standard subscribe CTA). The richer posts (VDI Drones, the F1 post, the AVD cheat sheet) follow a loose pattern: a personal hook, then a plainly-enumerated walkthrough of features/gear/specs, then a reader-value payoff. He deliberately signals when he is NOT going into full detail, trusting the reader to follow up.

## Humour
Self-deprecating and self-aware, especially about his own spending impulses and skill level ("I used to be hardcore, but that was 20 years ago, or so", "because I have some self-knowledge, I will probably want to upgrade ASAP"). Light, never cynical. Parenthetical asides carry most of the personality ("(a.k.a. performance guru's)", "(nice!)", "(a deep one)").

## Signature moves and recurring tells
- Inline parenthetical asides for personality and hedging.
- Smileys dropped straight into prose ( :) ).
- Ellipses and trailing "..." for conversational pauses ("Choices, choices...", "Autotune is probably one the coolest new features...").
- Elongated words for emphasis ("soooo smooth", "many, many").
- Sign-offs: "Till next time." on personal posts; "I hope this helps, thank you for reading!" on release/utility posts. Standard "download/subscribe" CTA on podcast posts.
- Credits real people by name and traces their community roots (Barry Schiffer, Dennis at VDI Drones, @AaronDinnage), reinforcing his insider positioning.
- Practical skepticism, e.g. checking whether "the Microsoft promised IOPS are indeed delivered by your Azure virtual machine."
- Honest expectation-management: "consider this document as a starting point", "It is by no means my intention to provide you a list of all security features available today."

## How he addresses the reader
Directly and conversationally, with rhetorical questions and "you"-framing ("right?", "first world problems!", "just download the .exe and you are good to go"). He assumes a knowledgeable peer audience and writes to them as equals.

## Shift from earlier writing
Volume has collapsed compared to the 2013-2020 cadence, and the heavy Citrix/EUC technical deep-dive (with "Reference materials used:" lists and "As per Citrix:" citations) has receded. The new positioning is Nerdio-aware but vendor-neutral ("with and without Nerdio"), and he openly ties technical observations back to his Nerdio day job ("just as with Nerdio, things move extremely fast"). What survives intact is the voice: parentheticals, smileys, self-deprecation, and the warm helper sign-offs. Typos and informal number formatting ("10.000") are left in, underscoring that this is unedited, authentic Bas.
