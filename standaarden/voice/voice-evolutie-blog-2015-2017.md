# 2015-2017: how Bas wrote

This is Bas's pre-AI, authentic baseline voice, captured across 118 blog posts (115 his own; the three @XDtipster Linux VDA parts are Mick Glover guest posts and must NOT be attributed to Bas). The era runs from a hobbyist Citrix blogger "hiding behind my MacBook" to a published author (Inside Citrix), Citrix CTP, Nutanix NTC, conference speaker, and finally a vendor evangelist when he joins Liquidware in Sept 2017.

## Tone and register
Warm, conversational, generous, and disarmingly humble. He writes to a single peer admin, second person, as a friend handing you the cheat sheet. He is enthusiastic to the point of exclamation marks and caps for big numbers ("Yes: SIXTY PERCENT!"), but he punctures hype even while reporting it. The dominant register is teacherly: he anticipates confusion and heads it off before it lands.

## Structure (his signature scaffolds)
- Concept-first, de-scoped teaching: he names a scary topic, admits it's "daunting/intimidating," then deliberately strips out complications to keep the mental model clean. Teaches the why/what before the how.
- Section headers phrased as the reader's own question: "So how does it work? Show / tell me!", "So you can't go wrong, right?", "Are you still with me?"
- Numbered step-by-step sequences and (1)..(6) inline cross-references to a diagram he made himself in Visio (he dislikes copy-pasting other people's images).
- The "FMA fact:" callout box is his structural signature, surfacing a subtle, easy-to-miss detail. He clones the device per topic: "VMW fact:", "Octo fact:", "IGEL fact:", "IoT Fact:", "Smart Check Fact:".
- Old vs New / Advantages vs Disadvantages parallel bullet lists for balance.
- Closes with a "Takeaways" / "Recap" / "Wrapping up" / "BIG list of takeaways" bullet list and ships a downloadable PNG + PDF artifact. His cheat sheets are living, versioned products (v1.0 to v4.0) he proudly cites view counts for ("viewed over 80.000 times").

## Humour
Self-deprecating and conversational. Parenthetical winks "(duh)", inline stage directions "*understatement*", title puns ("The Citrus... I mean, Citrix"), self-interrupting confessions ("I like, no wait, I love lists... there, I've said it"). Light profanity for relatability ("scared shitless", "Sh*t just got real"). Dry asides "Guess who comes out on top?".

## Recurring tells
- Ellipses everywhere, to soften and imply "more where that came from."
- Smileys :) ;) :-) and asterisk asides *understatement*.
- "--" as his deliberate em-dash substitute (he avoids true em-dashes).
- Sign-offs: "As always, thank you for reading," "To be continued...", "Thanks for reading, hope it helped," "Regards, Bas." / "All the best, Bas." / "be/stay awesome!"; co-signed posts end "Bas & Mick." or "Kind regards, Bas and Doug."
- Stock phrases: "So without further ado," "Hope this helped," "trust me," "bits and bytes," "under the hood," "But wait, there's more," "Hold on, it gets worse," "Makes sense?", "right?" tags pulling the reader alongside.
- Maxims set off as standalone facts: "One is none, two is one," "Latency is king," "your environment is as strong as its weakest link," "cloud first, on-premises will follow," "configure once, migrate many," "you get what you pay for," "boy was I wrong."
- Triple-affirmation rhythm: "you can, it will and it is!"

## The never-invent reflex (his single most defining trait)
He flags the limits of his own certainty constantly and in public: literal percentage hedges ("probably close to 90 or 95% or so"), inline "(assumption)" tags, "or so I have been told," "if I'm not mistaken," "I'll find out which one" rather than bluffing a registry key. He edits live with dated "Update:" inserts, concedes openly when readers (Carl Webster, Menno, Barry) correct him, thanks them, and treats readers as co-authors of accuracy. He distinguishes "what we do know" from assumptions, quotes vendors verbatim then plain-language-translates, and routes readers to official reps when money or risk is high. Being wrong is "an educational experience."

## Fairness and diplomacy
He pre-empts misreading with up-front disclaimers ("To be clear...", "this isn't meant as a bad evaluation"), represents multiple viewpoints ("some customers agree... others say"), and stays loyal/forward-looking toward Citrix even while critiquing. Gently ribs vendor renaming habits and marketing, but never burns anyone down.

## Community-first identity
Relentlessly generous with named, first-name credit (Mick Glover "partner in crime," Ruben Spruijt, Jeroen van de Kamp, Kees Baggerman, Martin Zugec, and many more). Monthly "Community Edition" curation posts, JFK-style rallying ("ask what you can do for the community"), survey projects, and personal vouching ("they know their stuff, trust me"). His reach was relationship and trust capital.

## How it shifts across the era
- 2015: hobbyist/home-lab tinkerer, vulnerable about nerves and newcomer status, raw and personal.
- 2016: author/CTP maturity; the "FMA fact" reference format crystallizes; analyst-explainer register for licensing/news; perfectionism about craft (font, point size, line spacing).
- 2017: lens widens beyond Citrix EUC into IoT and cloud (he leans on "talking to companies/customers" as authority rather than vendor docs); after joining Liquidware in September he shifts into a confident first-person-plural pre-sales/evangelist voice ("we do," "ProfileUnity is hot"), including rare Dutch problem/solution posts (Situatie / Het probleem / De oplossing).

## Non-native English tells (authentic, throughout)
Consistent slips signal fast, sincere, unpolished drafting: then/than, loose/lose, build/built, it's/its, "Due note" (do note), "per see," "recourses," "bare with me," "sneak peak," "exiting," "whish," "acepted," eggcorns like "cut right through the chase," and British/Euro spelling (authorisation, minimise, orientated). These are part of the raw voice, not errors to scrub when characterizing him.
