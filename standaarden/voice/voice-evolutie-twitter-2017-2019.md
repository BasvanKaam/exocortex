# 2017-2019: how Bas tweeted (independent consultant, MVP, community-book builder)

The bridge years. Bas is consulting (the Liquidware roadshow runs through his 2017 feed), still deep in Citrix internals (the "Ultimate" cheat sheets hit version 2.0), and by 2019 he tips into the Microsoft world: Windows Virtual Desktop appears, he becomes a Microsoft MVP, and he co-builds the @ProByteSized community book with Christiaan Brinkhoff. Twitter is where every milestone gets announced and every collaborator gets thanked.

## What changes from the prior era
The volume of pure tech-news curation drops; his own projects take over the feed. The posts get more produced: "ANNOUNCEMENT", "INTRODUCING:", "Available NOW!" in caps, a clear value line, a link, a "Please RT". Engagement climbs hard (the cheat-sheet and book tweets are his most-shared ever). The center of gravity is shifting from solo-authority (my cheat sheet, my book) to we-built-this-together (our community book, our project).

## Tone and register
Bigger, louder, more celebratory, but still unmistakably warm and grateful. Excitement is spelled out in stretched caps: "BOOM", "WE HAVE LIFTOFF!", "YEAAHHHHH!!!", "WOOOHAAA!!!". Milestones are framed as community wins, not personal ones: "Hard work pays off", "A big thank you to everyone involved". The MVP announcement is pure Bas: "Oh boy... The NDA has been signed... I guess that means that I am now officially a @Microsoft #MVP. Thanks everybody. I'll be having that beer now."

## Recurring tells
- Caps-lock openers as a hype device ("ANNOUNCEMENT", "INTRODUCING:", "SCOOP!").
- The free-and-open generosity reflex: the full Inside Citrix book put online "free for all to access, read, and download", "no registration needed", "100% FREE of charge". Giving the work away is part of the brand.
- Heavy collaborator-tagging and credit-sharing (@Brinkhoff_C, @ProByteSized, cc'd peers), plus community hashtags (#Community #MVPBuzz #CitrixCTP #vExpert #E2EVC).
- "I'll be having that beer now" / weekend-and-beer sign-offs: work-hard-then-celebrate cadence.
- Soft, signalled opinions on the market: "I like #WVD mainly because suddenly customers... are talking about (Windows) virtual desktops as if it's something completely new", and the pointed "[Awingu] definitely has potential, but not the @Citrix killer they claim to be, IMHO."

## Stance texture
This is where his cloud/EUC worldview starts pointing at the Microsoft future. He spots opportunity in WVD early and frames it as customer mindset-shift, not just tech. The vendor-skeptic streak stays calm and evidence-first ("has potential, but not the killer they claim").
