# 2012-2014: how Bas wrote

This is the authentic, pre-AI baseline. The file's earliest signal is March 2013, so this era is really the basvankaam.com Citrix-blogger years: 76 posts, March 2013 to December 2014. The voice is remarkably consistent across the whole stretch, an enthusiast technologist learning in public and bringing the reader along.

## Tone and register
Warm, conversational, self-deprecating. He writes like he is talking to a peer over a beer, not lecturing. The dominant move is humility-as-credibility: he openly states what he does not know ("I'm no networking kind of guy", "Since I'm no expert", "I'm no guru, I don't know everything and probably never will... but who cares?!"). He treats hitting errors in a lab as a feature of the writeup, not an embarrassment ("Fortunately I did run into some errors :-)"). Enthusiasm is unguarded and he pre-empts the charge of being over-eager ("I know, I get carried away sometimes"; "no, I'm not a Citrix employee and I don't get paid").

## Structure
Most posts follow a recipe: a personal or news hook to open, theory/terminology first (he loves to "keep it simple" and define acronyms in full on first use), then a screenshot-by-screenshot hands-on walkthrough narrated in present tense first person ("I selected", "Guess which one I picked?"), then a Conclusion / Round up, then a "Reference materials used:" line and the "Bas van Kaam ©" sign-off. He thinks in series and arcs, constantly cross-referencing his own earlier articles ("If you recall, and I think you do"), and frames cheat sheets as numbered "Note:" entries. Conversational section headers act as rhetorical beats ("Hold your horses", "But wait, there is more", "It doesn't end there").

## Humour
Dry, parenthetical, gently self-mocking. Jokes about install rituals ("you've clicked 'Next' seven times and hit 'Finish' the eight time"), his own Visio skills ("don't be to hard on me"), and his own typos. Sarcasm is light and signalled, often via scare-quotes ('basic', 'just', 'romantic', 'handicaps') or a winking smiley, never bitter even when he is critical of a vendor.

## How he addresses the reader
Second person throughout, treating the reader as a peer he is figuring things out alongside. Tag-questions everywhere ("right?!", "doesn't it?", "shall we?!", "don't you agree?"). He routinely turns a post into a crowdsourcing exercise ("correct me if I'm wrong", "I might need your help on this one!", "drop me a line"), and he means it: he updates articles based on comments and thanks commenters by name. Closers are warm imperatives ("Happy studying and good luck", "Good luck and have fun!", "till next time").

## Recurring tells
- Ellipses (…) as connective tissue and to imply the obvious ("well…").
- Emoticons mid-sentence: :-) for warmth, ;-) for a wink, :-( for sarcasm ("big fan of flying :-(").
- "?!" as his enthusiasm/surprise punctuation.
- "As per Citrix:" / quoting the vendor verbatim then adding his own read; "Reference materials used:" at the end.
- Catchphrases: "it all depends" (he even flags its return: "there it is again ;-)"), "no rocket science", "Don't shoot the messenger", "give it up already!", "bits and bytes", "keep it clean".
- The signature blend of tech and life: deep technical posts that end on a personal note about cycling Alpe d'Huez or the Tour de France ("Go Bauke!").
- "Bas van Kaam ©" sign-off; "Regards, Bas." in comment replies.
- Generous name-dropping and crediting of peers (Brian Madden, Kees Baggerman, Barry Schiffer, named Twitter handles).
- Authentic fast-publish texture: typos left in (than/then, of-course with a hyphen, apposed, andor) as part of the voice, not polished out.

## What is NOT here yet
No em-dashes (his later hard rule is already honoured naturally). No AI-smoothing: spelling and grammar are loose, sentences run on, asides interrupt themselves. The voice is unmistakably his and entirely human. If there is any drift across the two years it is toward slightly more assertive, opinionated myth-busting posts by late 2014 ("CL doesn't stand a chance!", "no HA, period") while keeping the same humility and reader-first instinct underneath.
