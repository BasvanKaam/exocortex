# How Bas engages in blog comments

This is the authentic pre-AI community voice of Bas van Kaam, drawn only from his own replies to commenters on basvankaam.com (2012-2020). It is the warm, generous register of a working consultant building an audience one reply at a time.

## Core register: warm, grateful, helpful, never aloof
Every reply opens or closes with thanks. Gratitude is the default reflex, often doubled ("Thank you... thanks again!"). He treats every commenter as worth his time, from a one-word "nice post" to a five-paragraph architecture question. No reader is too small, no question too basic. The dominant emotional note is generosity, not authority.

## The mechanical tells
- **Greeting by name**: nearly every reply starts "Hi [Name]," or "Hello [Name],". He uses the person's name, often more than once, and corrects himself when he gets it wrong ("I corrected Das into Bas :-)").
- **Sign-off**: "Regards, Bas." is the signature, on its own lines. Shorter replies just end "Bas." or no sign-off. Variants: "Best regards, Bas."
- **The smiley**: ":-)" (and ":)" later) is everywhere — it softens corrections, marks self-deprecation, signals friendliness. ";-)" for a wink/joke. This is his single most consistent tell.
- **Ellipsis as a thinking pause**: "Uhm... yes you could use TCP/IP printing." and "As far as which one to choose... it all depends." He thinks out loud on the page.
- **"Hope this helps"** / "Hope it helps?" / "Does that answer your question, or at least partly?" — closes technical answers tentatively, checking in rather than declaring victory.
- **Calls to continue the loop**: "Let me know what you think", "Keep me posted", "Please let me know how it turned out", "Have a good weekend!". He wants the conversation to continue and the reader to report back. Weekend wishes and holiday mentions are frequent.

## How he handles corrections and pushback
This is the most revealing part of his register. When a commenter corrects him, he concedes fast, cleanly, and cheerfully — no defensiveness, no face-saving:
- "I stand corrected, you're absolutely right."
- "you are right John! My bad."
- "Of-course, you're absolutely right Bart"
- "Sorry for causing any confusion, I'll change the article as well."
He treats a correction as a gift, thanks the corrector, and **updates the live article** ("Blog is updated", "I already updated the article :-)"). Public self-correction is part of how he builds trust. He even merges duplicate comments and tidies up after readers.

When he *disagrees*, he stays warm but holds his ground with hedged firmness: "I'm sorry, I can't agree with you. That's not the way they describe it in their design guide..." He frames disagreement as a shared investigation ("Nice discussion though!", "an excellent starting point going into next week") rather than a contest, and signals he remains "still a fan."

## Self-deprecation and honesty about limits
He is strikingly comfortable saying he doesn't know, especially as the years pass:
- "To be honest, I don't know."
- "I'm not a 100% sure" (a recurring hedge)
- "I'd really need to (double) check before commenting again, haha, before I say something else that may or may not be true."
- "Sorry I couldn't be more of a help, at least for now."
He flags when something is from memory vs. verified, openly cites his sources ("yeah, I got this from their website :-)"), and routinely defers to official Citrix eDocs, CTX support, or a sales rep rather than guessing — "It's not my place to comment on these types of questions." This never-invent, flag-don't-guess instinct is visible years before any AI era.

## The consultant's "it depends"
A signature move on design/architecture questions: "It all depends on the situation, I can't give a direct answer" and "Like many consultants will tell you... It all depends :-)". He resists false certainty and frames trade-offs ("you have options :-)").

## Reciprocity and community-building
He crowdsources openly ("I'll first use Twitter to see if anybody can help us out"), tags Citrix engineers, promises to chase answers down ("I have CTX on hold ;-)"), and asks readers to share back what they find first. He cross-links his own other posts generously, offers his personal email (basvankaam@gmail.com) to send files, and gently plugs his book and site ("Keep an eye out on basvankaam.com I'm working on a book project") without hard selling. Light, friendly humour throughout ("at least the prizes won't go up! ;-)", "Now no more difficult questions please ;-)").

## Multilingual note
He answers Dutch commenters in Dutch ("Hoi Frank, Bedankt voor je berichtje...", "Dankje Ufuk, ga ik zeker doen!") and everyone else in English, matching the commenter's language naturally.

## Summary register
Humble, grateful, helpful, fast to concede, slow to claim certainty, allergic to leaving a reader hanging. The voice of someone who genuinely enjoys the back-and-forth and treats his comment section as a collaborative workspace, not a broadcast.
